### Case for
It takes the surveillance asymmetry that defines platform capitalism and surgically reverses it: workers finally get the data-extraction tools that have always been pointed at them, turning Deliveroo's own rider logs into evidence of wage theft and algorithmic discrimination. The project is already winning in court—this isn't speculative potential, it's political technology that is materially redistributing power right now.

### Case against
Its victories remain legally bounded to UK/EU jurisdictions with relatively strong labor protections; the model hasn't proven it can survive transplant to gig economies where labor law is functionally absent. More fundamentally, it addresses symptoms of platform power rather than the underlying data-extraction business model—Deliveroo can simply change its terms of service faster than courts can rule.

### Why not the runners-up?
**GOV.UK Notify** — While the argument revealed its deeper stakes—whether a democratic state can build resilient communication infrastructure without replicating platform lock-in—it ultimately couldn't answer the concrete charge that carriers can unilaterally sever the government's own communication channels. The project remains vulnerable to the exact power asymmetry it claims to solve.

**OCCRP Aleph** — Though it democratizes access to investigative datasets, it remains fundamentally a tool for professional journalists rather than a redistribution of investigative capacity to the communities being investigated. The power to interpret and act on the data still flows through institutional gatekeepers.

**Wikidata** — The argument exposed that its apparent openness masks structural epistemic violence—the platform's foundational properties embed colonial jurisdiction models that make Indigenous knowledge literally uncomputable. The project requires not better rules but successful mutation of its axiomatic core, something none of the agents could demonstrate is actually possible under current governance.

**Portfolio argument:** Together these projects form a complete pipeline for 21st-century worker power: Worker Info Exchange extracts the evidence, Community Tech ensures workers can actually wield it, GOV.UK Notify provides legitimate state communication channels, Spartacus enables real-time collective action, and Murmurations scales the model across borders.
