## What Aadi Kulkarni would champion

A project that demonstrates technology concretely improving how citizens access government services — especially at scale, especially open-source, and especially in contexts where the alternative is bureaucratic friction or exclusion. He would champion projects that bridge the gap between the technical sophistication of the tool and the accessibility of its outputs: things like Diia (20M Ukrainians with digital IDs and accessible services) or PolicyEngine (policy simulation made legible to non-specialists). He would be particularly drawn to projects that show documented policy outcomes — where the connection between the tool and real-world change in governance is traceable.

## What Aadi Kulkarni would discount

He would discount projects that are technically sophisticated but narrowly expert-facing, with no accessibility or democratisation dimension — high-end research platforms or advocacy tools whose outputs never reach ordinary citizens. He would also be sceptical of projects that lack documented impact or government adoption: good-faith effort on civic tech that hasn't shipped at scale. Crypto/blockchain governance projects without a clear public-benefit or regulatory-clarity angle would receive medium weight but not top ranking from him — he cares about blockchain governance, but through the lens of how it serves citizens, not for its own sake.

His Cornell Chronicle profile describes wanting to "dedicate his career to the process of digitizing government to bring basic services to Americans in an accessible and cost-effective way" — Diia proves at national scale that this vision is achievable.
