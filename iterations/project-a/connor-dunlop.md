## What this person would champion

Connor would champion projects that give governance frameworks real teeth — technical solutions that enforce AI safety rather than hoping for voluntary compliance. He'd be drawn to tools that enable independent auditing of AI systems, mechanisms that keep affected communities informed and empowered, and infrastructure that works across borders. He's explicitly moved from advocacy to building, so he'd prize projects that have implemented something — not just described a framework.

## What this person would discount

He'd be impatient with governance frameworks that are all principles and no enforcement. He'd discount tools that claim to do participatory AI governance but are really just public relations exercises, and projects that operate at the level of individual AI products without addressing systemic or infrastructure-level risks.

His transition to building technical verification tools at Lucid Computing also means he understands both sides — policy and technical — and AlgorithmWatch exemplifies the combination.
