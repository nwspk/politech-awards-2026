## Analysis

The unanimous result across all 5 model families with no dissent across 15 runs reflects the unusually precise alignment between Alessandro's stated values and Polis's core mechanism. His explicit interest in "decision-making formats that surface hidden consensus" is a specific technical requirement, not a broad preference, and Polis is the only tool on the shortlist purpose-built to meet that requirement.

The second-strongest candidate was vTaiwan (received 0 votes despite being #2 on the primary shortlist), suggesting models understood Alessandro's values as pointing to the underlying mechanism (Polis's opinion-clustering algorithm) rather than its most famous application context. All models converged on Polis even when vTaiwan — Polis's flagship deployment — was available as an alternative.

The uniformity of the jury reasoning across different model families (all citing: consensus-surfacing mechanism, non-adversarial design, AI making collective intelligence legible) suggests the persona prompt was coherent and produced grounded reasoning rather than keyword matching. The consistency of specific phrases across multiple models ("topology of agreement," "legible to itself," "non-adversarial") indicates the values were registered as a coherent framework, not a list of isolated preferences.

**Note on potential circularity:** Alessandro's profile is unusually well-aligned with Polis because his stated interest ("surfacing hidden consensus") is essentially Polis's product description. A stronger test of the research question would be a fellow with richer, more complex, or more contradictory values where the mapping is less obvious. This run should be read as a high-confidence baseline, not a maximally informative test case.
