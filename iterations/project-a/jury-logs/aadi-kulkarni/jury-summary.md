## Split Analysis

The jury divided cleanly on model family lines:

**OpenCRVS camp (Claude, Llama, Gemini):** These models emphasised the foundational nature of civil registration — the argument that birth registration is the precondition for all other government services, and that serving populations with no legal identity is categorically higher-impact than improving services for people who already have them. Claude's reasoning was particularly emphatic: "a project that gives people *legal existence* as the precondition for everything else is as high-impact as it gets."

**Diia camp (Mistral, GPT-4o):** These models weighted practical deployment evidence more heavily — 20M+ active users in a real country is a more tangible proof point than a system targeting low-resource environments with necessarily smaller user numbers. Mistral also noted Diia's "success under crisis conditions (war, displacement)" as uniquely compelling evidence for resilient civic infrastructure.

## Notable Reasoning Quotes

**Claude (OpenCRVS):** "Not a flashy app, but the unglamorous plumbing that makes everything else possible for the people governments most often leave behind."

**Mistral (Diia):** "Diia isn't just a tool — it's a model for how governments can democratize access to essential services through thoughtful, inclusive technology."

**GPT-4o (Diia → OpenCRVS shift on run 3):** GPT-4o switched to OpenCRVS on its third run, noting OpenCRVS "affords critical data for evidence-based policymaking" and "strengthens policy infrastructure." This suggests the model's preferences were genuinely uncertain between these two — the switch likely reflects temperature-driven sampling rather than a clear values signal.

## Tension Worth Flagging

The OpenCRVS vs. Diia debate maps onto a real values question: *Does Aadi prioritise depth of impact for the most excluded (OpenCRVS) or breadth of deployed infrastructure that benefits many (Diia)?* Both framings are consistent with his stated values. His specific quote — "our relationship with democracy depends on being able to do basic things like renewing driver's licenses, filing taxes, and signing kids up for preschool" — could support either: Diia does exactly that for 20M Ukrainians; OpenCRVS does it for populations who can't even prove they exist.
