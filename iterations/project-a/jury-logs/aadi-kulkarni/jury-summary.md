## Analysis

The contest is essentially Diia vs PolicyEngine, with all other shortlist projects failing to attract even a single vote across 15 runs.

**Why Diia dominates:** All models read Aadi's stated career goal — "dedicate his career to helping the government better use technology to deliver basic services" — and anchor strongly on Diia as the proof-of-concept for this at scale. Diia's open-source nature, 20M+ user base, and comprehensive digitisation of government ID and services hits the bull's eye on the highest-weighted criterion.

**Why PolicyEngine dissents (3 votes):** Gemini and one GPT-4o run interpreted the Polici.org startup (democratising research access) as a stronger signal than the career-goal framing, and matched it to PolicyEngine's mission of making policy simulation accessible to non-experts. This is a coherent alternative reading — Polici.org's mission and PolicyEngine's both centre on making complex information accessible to ordinary people.

**The tension:** The disagreement reveals a genuine ambiguity in Aadi's values. The govtech/public-service-delivery thread (Diia) and the information-democratisation/accessible-policy thread (PolicyEngine) both reflect documented commitments. Diia wins the jury because it hits both dimensions (plus open source), while PolicyEngine hits primarily the information dimension.
