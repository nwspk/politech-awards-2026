## Analysis

The jury produced a **sharp 3-12 split**: Claude chose Aragon unanimously, while all four OpenRouter models chose Open Collective unanimously. This is a structurally meaningful divergence.

**Why Claude picked Aragon:** Claude's simulation leans into the Deutsche Bank + TreasureCorp combination and weights "programmable, on-chain governance" and "institutional-grade infrastructure" most heavily. Aragon ($35B+ governed) represents the proof-of-concept that on-chain governance can operate at institutional scale — which resonates with Huda's day job building custody infrastructure.

**Why four models picked Open Collective:** The other models appear to weight "transparent collective finance" and "community ownership" as the more universal expression of Huda's values — interpreting her interest in how communities control their money as better embodied by Open Collective's radical off-chain transparency than Aragon's on-chain mechanisms. They also appear to discount the Aragon weak point (partial open source, VC funding) more heavily.

**What this reveals:** The split maps cleanly onto the most interesting ambiguity in Huda's profile: is she primarily a *programmable governance* person or a *transparent collective finance* person? Both are genuine values, but they pull toward different winners. Claude's reading weighs her crypto-native background (Deutsche Bank DAC + Ethereum hackathons + TreasureCorp) more heavily. The other models weigh her stated interest in "how communities make decisions and build legitimacy" more broadly.

Given the 12/15 strong consensus, the jury winner is Open Collective. But this split is worth flagging for Huda's review — the question of which framing is correct is exactly what she's best positioned to answer.
