## Notable Reasoning

**Claude Sonnet 4.6 (run 1):** *"Loomio hits every structural test I care about: it was founded as a workers' co-operative, has spent over a decade building genuinely functional, self-hostable open source software, and its entire purpose is helping groups — cooperatives, movements, nonprofits — make collective decisions without concentrating power in a platform owner. **GPT-4o (run 1 — sole dissent):** Picked Decidim. Reasoning: *"Decidim embodies strong alignment with my values, offering a robust structural integrity with its nonprofit status, making its governance transparent and oriented toward public service. The platform empowers communities and municipalities worldwide to make collective decisions."* (Decidim is a reasonable runner-up — the dissent reflects genuine overlap between Loomio and Decidim under David's criteria.)

## What the Results Show

The near-unanimous convergence on Loomio (14/15) reflects how cleanly it maps to David Powell's stated values: the combination of co-operative origin, genuine open source, decade of operation without VC, and direct focus on group decision-making is rare in the list. Models consistently cited the structural alignment (not just the functional value) as the deciding factor — exactly the lens David's blog post articulates.

The sole dissent (GPT-4o run 1 → Decidim) is interesting because Decidim is the stronger choice on raw deployment scale and international governmental adoption, but doesn't carry the same cooperative worker-ownership DNA. GPT-4o appeared to weight breadth of civic deployment more heavily than structural ownership. On runs 2 and 3, GPT-4o corrected to Loomio — suggesting the dissent was near-borderline rather than a fundamentally different reading of David's values.
