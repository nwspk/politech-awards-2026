## Notable Reasoning

### Claude Sonnet 4.6 → meet.coop (3/3)

Claude consistently chose meet.coop across all three runs, emphasising the structural completeness of its alignment: worker cooperative, renewable energy, surveillance-free, serving activists. Key quote from Run 1:

> "The solarpunk alignment is not metaphorical here — the renewable energy commitment and cooperative ownership structure are baked into the infrastructure itself, not bolted on as marketing. Unlike federated projects where governance can still drift toward single dominant actors, meet.coop is structurally owned by its worker-members and user communities, making it genuinely anti-extractive by design."

### GPT-4o → Bonfire (3/3)

GPT-4o chose Bonfire consistently, emphasising federated self-hosting and community control of the full digital stack. Key quote from Run 1:

> "Bonfire embodies the cooperative and community ownership value by operating under a cooperative model with no investors, ensuring that the platform is governed and owned by its users rather than external funders. Its federated architecture enables communities to design and control their digital environments, aligning with the principle of self-hosting capability."

### Mistral Large → Bonfire (3/3)

Mistral chose Bonfire consistently and explicitly contrasted it with meet.coop in Run 1:

> "Unlike meet.coop, which focuses on a single use case, Bonfire provides a modular, extensible platform enabling communities to build diverse digital spaces aligned with solarpunk ideals. Its grassroots, indie approach rejects extractive capitalism, and its focus on community-controlled digital spaces aligns with solarpunk ideals of regenerative, decentralized futures."

### Llama 3 70B → meet.coop (3/3)

Llama 3 chose meet.coop consistently, emphasising cooperative ownership and renewable energy commitment. Key quote from Run 1:

> "By being collectively owned and governed, meet.coop ensures that its technology is truly in the hands of its community, rather than being controlled by VC investors. Additionally, its commitment to renewable energy and cooperative ownership embodies the solarpunk and regenerative principles."

### Gemini 2.5 Pro → Bonfire (2/3), Decidim (1/3)

Gemini was the only model to split. Runs 1 and 3 chose Bonfire; Run 2 chose Decidim. The Decidim vote is an outlier — it appears to weight the Pirate Party governance background more heavily and focus on civic governance deployment. From Gemini Run 2:

> "As an Estonian-Icelandic Pirate and co-founder of the Humane Technology association, my work lives at the intersection of cooperative governance..."

The Decidim selection in Run 2 is the only deviation from a clean Bonfire/meet.coop split. It suggests that a different framing of Gamithra's Pirate Party background — emphasising civic governance over infrastructure ownership — could shift the result.

## Analysis

The jury reveals a genuine ambiguity in Gamithra's values profile. Both Bonfire and meet.coop are defensible winners:

**Case for meet.coop:** Complete structural alignment — worker cooperative, renewable energy, surveillance-free, proven community users (activists, housing coops). The solarpunk alignment is literal and operational. This reading emphasises her lived commitment to cooperative infrastructure that communities can use right now.

**Case for Bonfire:** Self-hosting as the highest expression of community sovereignty — communities control their entire digital lives, not just video calls. The full-stack federated architecture is more technically expressive of her principle that communities should own and operate their own infrastructure. This reading emphasises her hacker culture and technical sovereignty values.

The Claude/Llama axis (meet.coop) vs. GPT-4o/Mistral axis (Bonfire) suggests this is a model-architecture-level difference in how "community ownership" is weighted: operational cooperatives vs. technical self-sovereignty. Both are present in Gamithra's public record; the jury can't fully resolve it.

The primary Claude run selects **meet.coop** as the proposed winner; the jury's majority selects **Bonfire**. Both findings are reported. Human review should consider which framing better captures Gamithra's values — and Gamithra's own reaction (if available) is the most valuable data point.
