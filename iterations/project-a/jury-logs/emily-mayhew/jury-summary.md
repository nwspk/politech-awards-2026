## Analysis

This is the most contested jury result in the Project A cohort so far. Three distinct projects each received significant support, reflecting a genuine ambiguity in how Emily Mayhew's values resolve across different scoring criteria.

**The three-way split reflects a real interpretive tension:**

1. **Open Contracting Partnership** (Claude 3x, Llama3 2x): Claude consistently picks OCP by reasoning that Emily's "cross-boundary, institutional pragmatism" lens, combined with her "fair and transparent systems" value, points to large-scale government procurement transparency as the highest-impact candidate. The reasoning is that $2 trillion in annual procurement is where these values have the most material consequence.

2. **Open Digital Planning** (GPT-4o 2x, Mistral 2x): GPT-4o and Mistral both emphasise "practical government applicability" in the most literal sense — Open Digital Planning is a UK-specific, council-built, council-deployed tool directly in the local government digital transformation space that Emily's bio identifies as a prior career area. These models read "practical" as "specific and local" rather than "large-scale and international."

3. **Creative Commons** (Mistral 1x, Llama3 1x, Gemini 2x): Gemini was notably drawn by the direct relevance to Emily's current DCMS role on creative content AI licensing. Gemini's two consistent Creative Commons picks and Mistral's third-run switch suggest that when the system prompt emphasised current role over career history, the creative licensing dimension dominated. Llama3's first-run Creative Commons pick was similarly motivated by the "protecting creative workers" value.

**Notable model reasoning quotes:**

- **Claude** (Run 3): "It's the kind of institutional infrastructure work — unglamorous, cross-boundary, rights-protective — that I think the sector consistently undervalues."

- **Mistral** (Run 1): "Open Digital Planning is a rare example of incremental institutional pragmatism — delivering tangible improvements within existing constraints, not just policy aspirations."

- **Gemini** (Run 1): "Its established framework for the open licensing of content is the foundational model for my work in building a fair and transparent marketplace for AI training data, addressing the vital need to protect creative workers."

- **Gemini** (Run 3): "The Data Trusts Initiative provides a tangible and governable framework to address the urgent challenge of responsible data sharing... Crucially, it offers a concrete path for protecting the rights of creators and citizens alike, which is central to my work at DCMS."

**Flag for human review:** This result is contested and should not be presented as a clear winner. The jury reveals a genuine interpretive ambiguity: should Emily's "civil servant background" lens prioritise her current role (creative content, copyright) or her career-wide portfolio (local government digital, procurement transparency)? These lead to different winners. Emily herself would be the best judge of which reading is correct.

## Discrepancy from Primary Run

The primary Claude run proposed **Open Digital Planning** as winner with reasoning emphasising the "built by practitioners for practitioners" dimension. The Claude jury runs (which included a slightly different framing with more emphasis on cross-institutional scale) consistently chose **Open Contracting Partnership** instead. This discrepancy within the same model family is itself a research signal — it suggests the ODP/OCP choice is genuinely close and sensitive to which aspect of Emily's background is emphasised.
