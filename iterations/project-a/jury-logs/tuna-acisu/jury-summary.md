## Notable Reasoning Quotes

### Claude Sonnet 4.6 (Run 1) — selecting HDX

> "HDX sits at the intersection of everything I care most about: open data infrastructure serving people in the global south and humanitarian contexts, real-world operational impact at scale, and data that directly shapes life-or-death decisions rather than influencing policy at the margins. With 1,000+ organisations relying on it as crisis response infrastructure, it represents exactly the kind of unglamorous but foundational data plumbing that enables evidence-based humanitarian action where it matters most. What distinguishes it from strong competitors like Gapminder or OpenCRVS is that it is simultaneously the infrastructure AND the content layer — it doesn't just set standards, it actively moves data to people who need it in the hardest operating environments on earth."

### Mistral Large (Run 1) — selecting HDX

> "HDX embodies the core of my values: global development data with direct humanitarian impact, open data infrastructure at scale, and practical policy utility in crisis response. By serving 1,000+ organizations, it bridges critical gaps in data accessibility for the Global South, where evidence-based decisions save lives. Its role as a trusted, neutral platform for sharing real-time data during emergencies aligns with my commitment to methodological rigour and public service. No other project on the list combines such global reach, humanitarian urgency, and open-data ethos — making it the standout choice."

### GPT-4o (Run 1) — selecting HDX

> "This project aligns closely with my values as it provides a crucial platform for humanitarian data sharing, supporting over 1,000 organizations in crisis response efforts. ### Llama 3 70B (Run 3) — the dissent, selecting Gapminder

> "My winner is Project 1: Gapminder Worldview Upgrader. This project resonates deeply with my values, particularly in countering misinformation and promoting data literacy. By providing a free educational tool to correct systematic misconceptions about global development, Gapminder Worldview Upgrader has the potential to empower individuals and institutions worldwide to make informed decisions based on accurate data. Its adoption by educational institutions in over 50 countries is a testament to its impact and scalability."

## Analysis

The consensus on HDX was unusually strong — 14/15 votes across 5 different model families. This consistency suggests that Tuna's values as inferred from her public work (global data infrastructure + humanitarian scope + open access) map cleanly onto HDX and that the mapping is robust across different model training lineages.

The single dissent (Llama 3 70B, run 3) picked Gapminder, which is the second-most plausible choice given Tuna's "countering misconceptions" work (her GitHub has a `media-news-deaths-analysis` repo and she co-authored "Does the news reflect what we die from?"). The Gapminder vote is not a weak signal — it reflects a genuine tension in Tuna's values between operational data infrastructure (HDX) and public-facing data education (Gapminder).

The primary Claude run agreed with the jury winner. No human review flag needed for consensus.

**Jury winner: Humanitarian Data Exchange (HDX)** — 14/15 votes, strong consensus.
