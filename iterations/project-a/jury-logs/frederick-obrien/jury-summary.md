## Analysis

The jury split is revealing. Three models (Claude, Mistral, Llama in 2/3 runs) converged on SecureDrop as the canonical expression of O'Brien's values — particularly the press freedom, independence, and free/open source dimensions.

GPT-4o diverged consistently toward Aleph and Alaveteli — both strong journalism tools, but GPT-4o appeared to weight journalistic *impact* (Panama Papers) and *scope* (FOI infrastructure) more than the source-protection and anti-capture angles that define O'Brien's Gonzo Engineering manifesto. The USAID funding controversy noted in the Aleph dossier was apparently either overlooked or considered less important by GPT-4o than the investigative impact.

Gemini's LittleSis pick in run 1 is the most distinctive outlier — it correctly identified O'Brien's "anti-exploitation of power" value but interpreted it through a different lens (power-mapping database vs. press freedom infrastructure). This suggests the "accountability and power exposure" criterion was being applied in a different hierarchy.

The 10/15 SecureDrop result (majority, not strong consensus) reflects genuine ambiguity in O'Brien's values: does he care more about the protection of individual sources (SecureDrop) or the systemic accountability infrastructure that investigative journalism produces (Aleph, LittleSis, Alaveteli)? His Gonzo Engineering manifesto suggests the former — individual practitioners and their craft — but the question is genuinely open.
