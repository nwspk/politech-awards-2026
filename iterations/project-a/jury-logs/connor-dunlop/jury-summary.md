## Analysis

The unanimity of this result is striking. For context, the previous fellow (Huda Abdirahim) produced a contested result (3 distinct winners across 15 votes). Connor Dunlop's values are so clearly delineated toward external AI accountability — and AlgorithmWatch so precisely matches those values — that there was no ambiguity for any model to resolve differently.

**Why AlgorithmWatch won so decisively:**
1. It addresses all three high-weight criteria simultaneously and explicitly: AI accountability, civil society participation, and regulatory information asymmetry reduction
2. The EU AI Act connection is specific and verifiable — exactly the legislation Connor spent 5 years working on
3. The anti-capture gGmbH structure eliminates the "voluntary compliance" concern that Connor has explicitly flagged
4. 17-country multilateral scope maps directly to his international governance value

**Why Framework for Meaningful Engagement 2.0 did not compete:**
The primary scorer (Claude, pre-jury) suggested it might be a runner-up, but the jury showed no hesitation. The Framework scores well on civil society participation but is a guidance document for developers rather than an independent accountability mechanism. Connor's values, as expressed in his interviews, emphasise *external accountability* — scrutiny from outside the developer — not developer-led engagement processes. This distinction was apparently clear enough to all five models.

**What this result suggests about the research question:**
Connor Dunlop's values are unusually well-defined and coherent in his public record. The unanimous jury result likely reflects this: the persona prompt was grounded in rich, specific, consistent public material, producing a well-defined values profile that leaves little room for model-specific variance. This contrasts with fellows whose public record is thin — where prompt ambiguity produces jury divergence.
