## New dossier fields added

**`citizen_accessibility_layer`** and **`citizen_accessibility_notes`** — added to 15 shortlist project dossiers.

**Schema:**
- `citizen_accessibility_layer`: `"yes"` / `"partial"` / `"no"` — whether the project has a participation layer accessible to non-specialist citizens without technical knowledge
- `citizen_accessibility_notes`: string — description of how the citizen-facing layer works or why it is absent

**Rationale:** Francesca's highest-weight criterion is accessible civic engagement — she explicitly cares about tools designed for ordinary citizens rather than policy professionals or technical specialists. The existing `movement_building_utility` classification distinguishes tool types but does not capture *who can use the tool without support*. This field directly maps to the gap.

**Projects updated (15):** Decidim, Polis, Humble Data Workshop, Talk to the City, TheyWorkForYou, Alaveteli, Participedia, Go Vocal, Citizen OS, Gapminder Worldview Upgrader, Full Fact AI, Organise, Missing Numbers, DoGooder, Open Data Editor (ODE).

**Examples:**
- Decidim: `citizen_accessibility_layer: yes` — "Purpose-built for citizen participation; no technical knowledge required to participate"
- Polis: `citizen_accessibility_layer: partial` — "Participation interface accessible to non-specialists; deployment requires technical knowledge"
- AlgorithmWatch: not updated (not in shortlist; field not added to projects outside shortlist scope)

## What Francesca Galli would champion

Francesca would champion tools that make civic participation beautiful, legible, and genuinely human — platforms built on commons principles where the architecture of participation is itself a political statement. She would particularly prize projects that invite people who don't see themselves as political activists into civic life; that bridge data with storytelling and design; that operate as genuine digital commons rather than extracting value from community energy; and that work for Italian and European diaspora communities, migrant populations, and people historically excluded from civic access. She would be especially drawn to tools that treat deliberation — the messy, uncomfortable, necessary work of talking across difference — as central rather than performative.

## What Francesca Galli would discount

She would discount purely technical tools with no accessible layer — governance platforms designed for policy professionals that require specialist knowledge. She would be sceptical of tools that claim civic purpose but operate on extractive or surveillance-capitalist logic: free-to-use but data-harvesting, or open in name but closed in governance. She would also discount tools focused narrowly on electoral mechanics (voter targeting, campaign analytics, A/B testing) without a deeper theory of civic culture — she cares more about the health of democratic discourse than campaign wins.

## Proposed winner (primary run)

**Decidim**

Decidim is the clearest expression of everything Francesca Galli cares about at the intersection of civic technology, democratic culture, and anti-extractive design. It is free, open-source, community-governed, and built not as a commercial product sold to governments but as a genuine commons — originating from Barcelona's grassroots participatory movement and governed by the Decidim community itself rather than a corporation or foundation with external interests. The fact that it has been adopted by Partito Democratico in Italy and municipalities across Spain, France, Finland, Brazil, and Japan means it operates at exactly the intersection of digital commons philosophy and centre-left civic practice that defines her political commitments. Its design philosophy — treating deliberation as architecture, participation as something beautiful rather than bureaucratic — directly mirrors her humanistic, interdisciplinary conviction that aesthetic choices in civic tools carry political meaning. For a data analyst who writes about civic knowledge for non-specialists and volunteers to help social organisations use data well, Decidim's explicit orientation toward ordinary citizens rather than policy professionals is decisive; it is the project she would most want to exist.
