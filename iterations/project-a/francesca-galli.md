## New dossier fields added

**Field:** `citizen_accessibility_layer`

**Schema:** String — `"yes"` / `"partial"` / `"no"` / `"not_applicable"`. Optional companion field `citizen_accessibility_notes`.

**Definition:** Does the project have a non-specialist-facing interface or output layer designed for ordinary citizens (not just developers, researchers, or policy professionals)? "yes" = purpose-built for public/citizen use; "partial" = has citizen-facing components alongside technical layers; "no" = primarily professional/institutional; "not_applicable" = infrastructure or research-only.

**Rationale:** Francesca's values centre on civic accessibility — the gap between "open source" (technically available) and "actually usable by a person attending a local PD branch meeting" is a real dimension not captured by existing fields.

**Projects updated:** vote-for-policies, polis, decidim, talk-to-the-city, theyworkforyou, gapminder-worldview-upgrader, full-fact-ai, dogooder, go-vocal, humble-data-workshop, missing-numbers, participedia, new-public-roundabout, organise, alaveteli

## What Francesca Galli would champion

Francesca would champion projects that make civic participation beautiful, legible, and genuinely human — tools that connect data to stories people can feel, platforms that invite participation from people who don't see themselves as political activists, and civic tech built with aesthetic care and solidarity values. She would particularly prize projects rooted in European civic traditions (deliberative democracy, municipalism, collective memory) and tools that demonstrate the commons argument in action: shared ownership, transparent governance, no extraction from civic energy. The combination of DataKind volunteering and PD activism suggests she is especially drawn to the crossover between data communication for social good and grassroots political organising.

## What Francesca Galli would discount

She would be impatient with purely technical tools that have no accessible layer — governance platforms designed only for policy professionals, data dashboards that generate numbers without narrative or civic meaning. She would be sceptical of projects that claim civic purpose but extract value from community data without accountability, or that require specialist knowledge to operate. She would also be cool towards civic tech that is politically neutral to the point of meaninglessness — she has explicit values, and tools that refuse to acknowledge power asymmetries may feel evasive rather than principled.

## Proposed winner (primary run)

**Decidim**

Decidim is the most precise match for Francesca Galli's particular combination of values. It emerged from Barcelona's municipalist movement — a living expression of European centre-left participatory democracy — and is built as genuinely free, open-source infrastructure that refuses to extract value from civic energy. As a visual artist and careful writer, Francesca would appreciate that Decidim takes the *design* of civic space seriously: its interface is a political argument, not just a utility, treating deliberation as something worth doing beautifully. It maps directly onto her PD London context (Partito Democratico in Italy actively uses Decidim for internal party deliberation), her DataKind conviction that data should serve social good not corporate extraction, and her Substack's commons-based critique of digital platforms. The fact that it is deployed by real municipalities, political parties, and grassroots organisations across Europe means its civic theory has been tested in messy pluralistic reality — not as a prototype or a dashboard, but as actual democratic infrastructure.
