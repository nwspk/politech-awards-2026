## What this person would champion

Davit would champion tools that help ordinary people detect when they are being manipulated — especially in political contexts where the manipulation is systematic and state-backed. He'd be excited by platforms that make propaganda visible, measurable, and counterable even in resource-scarce settings. He'd also value tools that support the psychological resilience of activists and communities under authoritarian pressure.

## What this person would discount

He'd be deeply sceptical of projects that address disinformation only in well-resourced Western contexts, or that require significant infrastructure to deploy. He'd dismiss tools that are purely theoretical without practical application, and would be critical of "neutral" platforms that enable authoritarian manipulation to operate unchallenged.

## Proposed winner

**DISARM Frameworks**

The DISARM Frameworks are the most direct match for Davit Jintcharadze's research programme and lived political experience. He has co-founded a political party under authoritarian pressure, organised 190+ days of protest against election fraud, and is conducting academic research specifically on how authoritarian regimes use technology for opinion engineering. DISARM is the most rigorous open-source infrastructure for describing, categorising, and countering disinformation and information operations — it is the ATT&CK framework equivalent for influence operations. It is designed to work across contexts, can be adapted for LMICs (Georgia included), and is used by civil society organisations facing exactly the threats he has personally experienced. His stated goal is to "link disinformation narratives to psychological biases" — DISARM's TTP taxonomy provides the structural foundation for exactly this kind of analysis. No other project in the list so directly enables the research he wants to do and the tools he wants to build.
