## What Fatima Sarah Khalid would champion

Fatima would champion projects that are genuinely open source, community-governed, and deployed as public infrastructure — tools where the developers who build them are the same community who depend on them. She would be particularly excited by projects that make civic participation accessible to non-experts (citizens, public servants) while remaining extensible for civic hackers and developers, and that treat governance of the tool as seriously as the tool's governance mission. She has a special interest in projects at the AI governance intersection — not AI as a gimmick but AI with accountability structures, transparency mechanisms, and genuine public ownership.

## What Fatima Sarah Khalid would discount

She would discount proprietary platforms with civic-sounding missions but no community governance, no open codebase, and no developer ecosystem — tools where "open" is a marketing claim rather than a commitment. She would be sceptical of projects that treat inclusion as a feature to add later, of AI tools with no governance framework, and of civic tech that requires expensive contracts or lock-in to national vendor ecosystems. Disinformation counter-tools built on ad-tech infrastructure would score poorly. Tools that help campaigns but provide no transparency over their own operation would also score low.

## Proposed winner (primary run)

**Decidim**

Decidim represents the fullest expression of every value Fatima Sarah Khalid has demonstrated across a decade of civic tech and open source work: it is genuinely open source (Apache 2.0), democratically governed through the Decidim Free Software Association (a community body of developers, activists, consultancies, researchers, and public servants — not a vendor), and deployed by hundreds of governments as participatory infrastructure — from Barcelona's citywide strategic planning process to the European Commission's Conference on the Future of Europe to Brazil's federal participatory platform. Unlike many "civic tech" tools that layer participation onto existing commercial infrastructure, Decidim was architecturally designed to be community-owned and public-interest from inception: its governance model, funding structure (grants and public funding), and contributor community all reflect the same values Fatima articulates when she describes open source as a civic commons with responsibilities. The project is also developer-accessible in exactly the way a civic hacker would care about — a well-documented API, active contributor community, internationalization across many languages, and a track record of deployment in resource-constrained municipal contexts. If Fatima's Newspeak House research on AI governance leads her to design governance frameworks for AI systems in public procurement, Decidim's governance model is the closest existing analogue in democratic participation technology.
