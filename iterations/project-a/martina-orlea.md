## What Martina Orlea would champion

Martina would champion tools that directly help democratic movements win against disinformation, algorithmic manipulation, and asymmetric information warfare. She'd prize projects that translate into operational advantage in real campaigns — not just research outputs or technical demonstrations — and that are accessible to underfunded democratic movements globally, not just well-resourced Western institutions. She'd be especially drawn to tools that address the information environment holistically: giving campaigners and civil society a structured, practical framework for understanding and countering the disinformation tactics that authoritarian-backed and oligarchic political actors deploy. Projects that work in Romania, Ukraine, Georgia, Sri Lanka, or other contested democratic environments would score higher with her than tools designed exclusively for the UK or US context.

## What Martina Orlea would discount

She'd discount tools oriented toward deliberative or consultative processes divorced from electoral outcomes — platforms that engage citizens in discussion without influencing who wins. She'd be sceptical of academic research outputs that don't translate into operational tools campaigners can actually use under time pressure. Anything requiring significant institutional infrastructure, SaaS fees beyond what volunteer-run campaigns can afford, or deep technical expertise to deploy would score low. She'd also discount tools primarily serving incumbents, government institutions, or well-resourced NGOs rather than underdog democratic movements.

## Proposed winner (primary run)

**DISARM Frameworks**

Martina Orlea's most explicitly stated interest at Newspeak House is "analysing/fighting information warfare" — and DISARM Frameworks is the professional-grade, open-source toolkit specifically built for that work. Where other projects address symptoms (specific fake news stories, individual deceptive ads), DISARM provides a systematic taxonomy of disinformation tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs) that enables campaigners, civil society, and governments to identify and counter information operations at the strategic level — before they metastasise into electoral outcomes. It is the industry standard used by NATO, EU institutions, and election security practitioners globally, which means its adoption is not theoretical; it is the shared language of the organisations trying to protect democratic elections right now. Most critically for Martina's values, it is open-source and free — meaning a campaign team in Bucharest defending against oligarchic disinformation can use the same framework as a well-resourced Western government, directly addressing her conviction that democratic movements need access to the tools that their better-funded opponents already have.
