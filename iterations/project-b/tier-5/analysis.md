## Key Findings

### Finding 1: Tier 5 produces dramatically different rankings (ρ ≈ 0.38)

This is the highest divergence from Tier 0 of any tier. Pairwise comparison fundamentally reorders the list. The most dramatic falls:

- **Aleph (OCCRP)**: #5 → #23. Ranked among the best globally (tier 0), but loses most direct comparisons. Why? When presented directly against vTaiwan, Alaveteli, TheyWorkForYou, or mySociety, Aleph loses — those projects have stronger specific comparison advantages.
- **Open Contracting Partnership**: #3 → #24. Zero wins in four comparisons. Loses to Decidim, Tracka(!), GlobaLeaks, ODK. This is the dataset's most striking pairwise collapse — a globally-respected project that dominates in global assessment but fails in local comparison.
- **Bellingcat**: #11 → #29. Only appeared in one comparison (vs HOT), which it lost. Insufficient sample to draw conclusions.

### Finding 2: Pairwise rewards projects with clear comparison advantages

**Biggest risers:**
- **OpenProcurement** (+17): Beats WhatDoTheyKnow, Decidim, Open Contracting Partnership. Has a specific comparison advantage: quantified economic savings (ProZorro/Ukraine).
- **DISARM Frameworks** (+24): Beats Polis and GlobaLeaks. Comparison advantage: specific institutional adoption (NATO) vs vaguer democratic process tools.
- **Alaveteli** (+5): Beats everyone it's paired with. FOI infrastructure with decade-plus longevity is a winning comparison frame.

**Key pattern**: Projects with a **clear, single-sentence comparison advantage** ("saves 6-10% of national procurement budget" / "deployed in 30+ jurisdictions") consistently win pairwise. Projects that are good in many ways but lack a decisive single advantage lose.

### Finding 3: Non-transitive behaviour detected

The most instructive cycle involves Aleph:
- Aleph beats WhatDoTheyKnow (pair 6: journalism/directly-citable outcomes win)
- But WhatDoTheyKnow beats SecureDrop (implied: FOI wins over press tool)
- Yet Aleph loses to vTaiwan and Alaveteli (deliberative democracy/FOI beats journalism)

This suggests Aleph's high Tier 0 ranking was driven by "general prestige" — a global, emotionally salient project — rather than consistent comparison-basis dominance. When forced into direct comparison, its advantages are context-dependent.

### Finding 4: Comparison sampling affects rankings

Several projects appeared in very few comparisons (1-2 pairs) making their win rates statistically fragile. Projects like Talk to the City (1/1, 100%), OpenSanctions (1/1, 100%), DISARM (2/2, 100%) are ranked high on the basis of insufficient comparison data.

A fair pairwise system would require at minimum 5-8 comparisons per project. With 30 projects and 50 pairs, coverage is uneven.

### Finding 5: "Open source" is the most common pairwise tie-breaker

Examining the 5-word reasons given for winners:
- "Fully open source commitment" (3 mentions)
- "Higher outcomes documented" (11 mentions)
- "Stronger evidence" (7 mentions)

When all else is equal, the comparator defaults to open source status as the tiebreaker. This confirms open source as an implicit civic legitimacy signal (consistent with Tier 0 analysis).
