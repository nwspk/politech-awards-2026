## What Changed from v2 and Why

Three targeted changes to the v2 constitution, all driven by Huda's direct feedback on v2 results:

### Change 1: C3 (Collective Ownership) reweighted 20 → 12 pts

In v2, collective ownership was the dominant filter — equal weight to C1 (budget transparency) and C2 (governance legibility). Huda's feedback: collective ownership is an exploration space and interest, not a hard requirement. Strong ownership still matters but should not dominate the ranking.

Effect: Pure DAO/ownership infrastructure (Aragon, The DAO Framework, Tech Coops List, CharmVerse, Bonfire) dropped significantly. Tools that scored on C3 alone but lacked participation leverage fell most sharply.

### Change 2: M1 (Programmable Governance) expanded to off-chain democratic software

In v2, M1 fired only for on-chain/cryptographic mechanisms — explicitly excluding traditional civic tech. Huda's feedback: the on/off-chain distinction is not meaningful. What matters is whether governance is enforceable and legible in practice.

Effect: The M1 boost (+8–12 points) now fires for Decidim, CONSUL Democracy, Loomio, Citizen OS, LiquidFeedback, Ethelo, Cobudget, Polis — all of which use programmable democratic governance mechanisms in practice. This lifted the entire participatory democracy tooling tier significantly.

### Change 3: New C8 (Decision-Making Leverage) added at 8 pts

C3's freed 8 pts went entirely to a new criterion: does participation lead to real outcomes? Huda's direct framing: "what I care about more is whether people actually have leverage over outcomes." The vTaiwan case was the explicit motivator — it enables participation that feeds into real legislative decisions.

Effect: vTaiwan, CONSUL Democracy, participatory budgeting tools, and deliberative democracy platforms with documented government adoption all gained significantly. Pure consultation tools without binding outcome pathways were correctly penalised.

### The vTaiwan Case (the motivating gap in v2)

In v2, vTaiwan scored 50.0 at rank 68 — near-bottom despite being one of the most significant participatory democracy implementations globally. The v2 constitution penalised it for:
- Not having collective/DAO ownership (C3 was 20 pts, vTaiwan scores ~1)
- M1 not firing (on-chain only filter — vTaiwan uses off-chain tools like Pol.is)
- No budget transparency function

In v3:
- C3 reweighted down — vTaiwan's low C3 matters less
- M1 now fires for vTaiwan (+10 pts)
- C8 fires strongly at 8/8 — documented policy outcomes (Uber regulation, Airbnb regulation), government partnerships (Taiwan Executive Yuan), causation_strength = "documented"

Result: vTaiwan moves from rank 68 to rank 22 — a 46-place gain. Score goes from 50.0 to 51.2 (modest score gain because C1 and C3 still don't fire — vTaiwan isn't a budget transparency or collective ownership tool). The rank gain is larger than the score gain because many tools that scored above vTaiwan in v2 via C3 or M1 (on-chain only) dropped when those weights shifted.

## Does v3 Better Capture Huda's Stated Priorities?

**Yes, substantially.**

The v2 constitution had a structural bias toward DAO/on-chain infrastructure that didn't match Huda's broader concern. Her primary interest is in governance that is *enforceable and legible in practice* — and in participation that has *actual leverage over outcomes*. v2's constitution over-indexed on the technical substrate (on-chain) rather than the functional properties.

The v3 top tier now reads as a coherent expression of Huda's values:
1. **Ethelo** — governance legibility + programmable democratic mechanisms + real deliberation infrastructure
2. **Cobudget** — participatory budgeting + democratic allocation + programmable governance
3. **Citizen OS** — participatory democracy platform + government deployment
4. **LiquidFeedback** — liquid democracy + programmable governance
5. **Parti** — participatory platform with documented outcome pathways
6. **Loomio** — governance legibility + programmable mechanisms + real deployment
7. **CONSUL Democracy** — participatory democracy with government adoption
8. **Decidim** — off-chain democratic software + M1 fires + real deployment

This top 8 is a coherent cluster: all are participatory democracy tools with programmable governance and deployment evidence. The old top (Aragon, The DAO) were technically sophisticated but lacked the participation-to-outcomes pathway that Huda cares about most.

**The winner in v3 is Ethelo** at 68.9 — a deliberative decision-support platform with strong governance legibility, programmable democratic mechanisms, and documented participation infrastructure.

## Residual Limitations

1. **C1 (budget transparency) still dominates** at 20 pts — but few tools in the longlist are explicitly about budget transparency, so this criterion tends to create a floor rather than a ceiling.

2. **vTaiwan rank 22** — still lower than its significance might suggest. The reason is legitimate under this constitution: vTaiwan doesn't do budget transparency (C1 = 2) and doesn't have collective community ownership (C3 = 1). Given Huda's constitution, this is correct — vTaiwan's strength is participation-to-outcomes, which C8 now captures, but it's not a treasury transparency or collective ownership tool.

3. **Scoring methodology is heuristic** — the keyword-matching approach means projects with richer dossiers in English have an advantage. This is mitigated by underdog protection but not eliminated.
