### Criterion 1: Budget and treasury transparency as civic infrastructure

- **Weight:** high (20 points)
- **Why Huda:** The most explicitly stated value in the bio is "budget transparency is a precondition for legitimate decision-making." TreasureCorp's entire design premise — making treasury data legible, auditable, and analytically accessible — is a direct expression of this value in code. [bio, CONFIRMED; TreasureCorp website, CONFIRMED]
- **High score:** Projects that make public spending, institutional budgets, collective resource allocation, or governance finances directly visible, machine-readable, and actionable for accountability purposes. Infrastructure that enables citizens, community members, or governance participants to see where money goes and challenge it if needed. Tools that treat financial transparency not as a reporting requirement but as a democratic precondition.
- **Low score:** Projects that improve efficiency of spending decisions without increasing legibility to those affected. Internal financial tools that serve organisational convenience. Projects where the transparency benefit flows only to administrators, not to communities or governance participants.
- **Dossier fields:** political_relevance_summary, scraped_description, communities_served, primary_users_or_beneficiaries, issue_area

### Criterion 2: Governance legibility — making power and decision-making visible

- **Weight:** high (20 points)
- **Why Huda:** The bio states explicitly: "code shapes power and that relationship needs to be made visible and accountable." TreasureCorp tracks governance proposals and voting patterns — making governance activity auditable is a design choice, not incidental. Interest areas include "programmable governance" and "DAO decision-making." [bio, CONFIRMED; TreasureCorp website product feature, CONFIRMED]
- **High score:** Projects that make decision-making processes explicitly visible to those affected. Who has power, how it is exercised, what choices were made, who voted how. Tools that expose governance architectures — not just outputs but processes. Projects that enable accountability through visibility rather than through appeal to authority.
- **Low score:** Projects that automate governance without making it more legible. Decision-support tools that recommend without explaining. Platforms that increase efficiency of decision-making without increasing transparency about whose interests are served.
- **Dossier fields:** political_relevance_summary, scraped_description, issue_area, primary_users_or_beneficiaries

### Criterion 3: Collective ownership and community governance of infrastructure

- **Weight:** medium (12 points)
- **Why Huda:** "Collective ownership of resources should be technically legible and democratically governed" is the constitutional core of the bio. This is not a generic equity claim — it is a specific claim about how resources (including digital/financial resources) should be governed. The TreasureCorp product addresses the practical gap that collective ownership creates (DAO members who need to understand and govern shared treasuries). [bio, CONFIRMED] Reweighted in v3 based on direct feedback: collective ownership is an exploration space and interest, not a hard requirement. Strong ownership still helps but is no longer the dominant filter.
- **High score:** Projects owned or governed by their communities — cooperatives, DAOs, community land trusts, open commons. Projects where governance rights are distributed to those who use or depend on the infrastructure. Projects that make collective ownership technically viable rather than aspirationally described.
- **Low score:** Projects that serve communities without giving them ownership or control. Platforms that extract value from collective use without redistributing governance rights. Tools that are open-source in code but centralised in decision-making.
- **Dossier fields:** scraped_description, political_relevance_summary, communities_served

### Criterion 5: Interoperability and open standards

- **Weight:** medium (12 points)
- **Why Huda:** TreasureCorp's architecture prioritises public on-chain data — a design choice that relies on open, standardised data layers rather than proprietary APIs or closed data systems. Interest in "programmable governance" implies a preference for composable, open infrastructure over walled gardens. [TreasureCorp website, PROBABLE; bio, CONFIRMED]
- **High score:** Projects built on open standards, open data formats, or interoperable protocols. Tools that can connect to or be extended by other civic tech. Platforms that treat interoperability as a principle rather than a commercial feature.
- **Low score:** Proprietary platforms with closed APIs. Tools that create lock-in without providing portability. Siloed infrastructure that cannot connect to adjacent systems.
- **Dossier fields:** scraped_description, political_relevance_summary, issue_area

### Criterion 7: Legitimacy and accountability — is the project itself accountable?

- **Weight:** low (6 points)
- **Why Huda:** "Accountability" appears twice in the bio — "code shapes power and that relationship needs to be made visible and accountable" and interest in "legitimacy." A person building accountability tools is likely to apply an accountability lens to the projects they evaluate. Does the project itself practice what it preaches? [bio, CONFIRMED]
- **High score:** Projects that are themselves transparent about how they work, who funds them, what their governance model is. Open-source code, published methodology, public records of decisions.
- **Low score:** Projects that advocate transparency externally while being opaque about their own operations. Projects with unclear funding, unaccountable leadership, or no published governance model.
- **Dossier fields:** scraped_description, political_relevance_summary

### Criterion 8: Decision-Making Leverage — does participation lead to real outcomes?

- **Weight:** medium (8 points)
- **Why Huda:** Direct from feedback: "what I care about more is whether people actually have leverage over outcomes." She agreed vTaiwan should score higher — "even without strong ownership or treasury transparency, vTaiwan enables participation that feeds into real decisions. and that matters more to me." She proposed "effective participation" or "decision making leverage" as a core criteria.
- **High score (6–8):** Tools where participation demonstrably feeds into binding or consequential decisions. Participatory democracy tools with government adoption. Systems where the output of collective input has a documented pathway to affecting outcomes.
- **Medium score (3–5):** Tools that enable genuine participation with some pathway to influence, but binding outcomes are not guaranteed or documented.
- **Low score (0–2):** Tools that collect participation signals with no clear mechanism for those signals to affect outcomes. Consultation theatre. Reporting without action.
- **Dossier fields:** political_relevance_summary, policy_outcomes, government_partnerships, communities_served

**Criteria total maximum: 102 points. Normalise by dividing by 1.02 before applying modifiers.**
