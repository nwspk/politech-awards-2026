**The pattern:** Projects that present technical improvements to institutional operations as politically neutral — or, worse, as empowerment — without examining who benefits from that efficiency, who is excluded, and what power relations the design embeds. This is the M6 pattern, and it runs through the entire bottom of the ranking. ## 3. Constitutional Failure Mode

The v3 constitution has a structural tension that the ranking exposes more sharply than v2.

**The C4/C2 joint-high-weight structure creates a ceiling effect for well-documented, well-regarded civic tech institutions.** With C2 and C4 together constituting 49% of the raw criteria score, a project that does excellent structural work but has not publicly interrogated its own power dynamics is systematically capped. This is intentional — but it means that several large, genuinely impactful, widely-respected platforms (Decidim, Consul, Ushahidi, HOT) cluster in the 70-75 range rather than competing for the top positions, because their public documentation does not demonstrate the kind of C4 critical self-examination that the constitution rewards.

Alexandra would likely agree this is correct — and also acknowledge that it may be unfair to projects where the critical self-examination is happening internally but is not documented publicly. The traceability requirement partially addresses this by distinguishing *undocumented reflexivity* from *absent reflexivity*, but for large platforms the absence of public critical self-examination is harder to explain away.

**A second tension**: The M7 modifier (intra-class dynamics, +3–6) is the weakest signal in the constitution, but it accounts for why Worker Info Exchange and Turkopticon pull ahead of structurally similar projects. Both analyse not just that workers are excluded, but how the class position of employers, platforms, and trade-secret law beneficiaries reproduces that exclusion. Projects that lack this analytical layer — that name excluded populations without examining who benefits from their exclusion — are constitutionally disadvantaged relative to projects that do both. The v3 constitution encodes this as a modifier because Alexandra's feedback named it explicitly; but it may deserve criterion-level weight in a future iteration.

**A third observation**: The Gap 3 identified in the constitution (no criterion that directly rewards analysis of *who benefits from exclusion*, not just who is excluded) manifests in the mid-ranking cluster. Several housing, data, and civic accountability projects that do important work on named power asymmetries score in the 55-65 range because their analysis centres the excluded population but does not trace the intra-class dynamics of who profits from their exclusion. Alexandra would see these projects as doing valuable work that stops one analytical step short.
