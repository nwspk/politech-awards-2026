## Part C: Procedural Rules

**Abstention threshold:** Score N/A only when the dossier provides insufficient evidence to assess BOTH high-weight criteria (C2 — excluded populations, and C4 — critical self-examination) AND the project's accessible web presence also fails to establish any basis for either criterion. Thin dossier with any substantive field populated = score with HIGH uncertainty and apply underdog protection if completeness < 0.4. Abstain only when there is genuinely no basis for any scoring judgment.

**Prototype handling:** Prototypes receive partial protection on C6 (democratic depth) IF: (a) the project addresses a significant power asymmetry or centres excluded populations, AND (b) the design demonstrates genuine collaborative or empowering intent with a described mechanism. Eligible prototypes: C6 maximum capped at 8 pts. Theory of democratic engagement can substitute for up to 60% of C6 score IF it names how empowerment or collaboration would operate in practice (not aspirationally). Prototypes with no described mechanism receive no C6 protection.

**Popularity discount:** Documentation richness and civic tech prominence are not quality signals. For well-known projects: flag popularity_risk HIGH, note estimated score without dossier richness advantage (−8–12 pts). Do not automatically reduce. Additional caution: many high-profile "empowerment" and "transparency" tools are subject to M2 scrutiny and C4 (critical self-examination). Being well-known as an empowerment tool does not exempt a project from examination of whether its framing is doing institutional legitimation work.

**Tie-breaking:** (1) More specific named excluded population + named exclusion mechanism (C2)? (2) Documented lifecycle engagement across more stages (C3)? (3) Higher democratic depth level — empowers or collaborates vs. informs (C6)? (4) More directly corrects a named, significant power asymmetry (C1)? (5) More structurally disadvantaged as a project (C5)? (6) If still tied: higher dossier_completeness.

**Uncertainty handling:** Thin evidence does not lower scores. Completeness < 0.4 AND domain within core concerns → underdog protection applies (floor 30 pts). HIGH uncertainty noted in rationale but does not mechanically reduce score.

**Traceability requirement:** Every project score must be accompanied by a traceability entry. For each criterion: (a) dossier field(s) used as evidence; (b) specific content of that field; (c) interpretation — why this constitutes evidence for or against the criterion; (d) score assigned; (e) justification. Partial scores must be explicitly justified. Modifier applications must record trigger condition and magnitude applied. Required for all 321 projects.

**Novelty vs democratic depth:** Theory of democratic engagement can substitute for up to 60% of C6, but only when it names how empowerment or collaboration would operate in practice (what decisions, by whom, via what process). Generic aspiration does not qualify.

**Movement infrastructure vs direct service:** Neither systematically privileged. C2 and C4 tend to favour projects with direct community engagement. Movement infrastructure scoring higher on C1, C3, C6 is structural, not a deliberate de-prioritisation.

**Scope of concern:** Geographic scope not a primary determinant. Global South and non-Western projects not disadvantaged. Apply values with appropriate uncertainty when context differs significantly from her evidence base (UK/Romanian housing, justice, climate).

## Synthesis Notes

### The C4/C2 joint-high-weight structure

With C4 raised to 20 pts and C2 remaining at 20 pts, these two criteria together constitute 40 of 82 available points (49% of the raw score). This means a project must score well on both to compete for top positions — being excellent at centring excluded populations (C2) without critical self-examination of its own power claims (C4) will score medium at best. This is intentional and reflects Alexandra's revised feedback: the most important question is not just who you serve, but whether you have examined whether your design actually serves them in the way you claim.

### The reduced C1 weight

C1 (power asymmetry addressing) drops from 20 to 12 pts. This means strong accountability infrastructure alone no longer wins. A well-designed contestability mechanism that fails to centre specific excluded populations (C2) and fails to examine its own power claims (C4) will score medium. The intent: accountability infrastructure is necessary but not sufficient — it must be grounded in whose power asymmetry is being addressed and whether the project has been honest with itself about whether its mechanism actually redistributes power.

### C5 as a fairness correction for the awards process itself

C5 (structural underdog, 6 pts) is not a capability signal — it is an explicit fairness correction. It reflects Alexandra's awareness (evidenced through her ethnographic consultancy and her own research) that the most important work is often the least documented. Applied to the awards process: a grassroots project doing critical work with Roma communities may produce a thin dossier; a well-funded civic tech organisation with professional communications staff may produce a rich one. C5 partially corrects this. It is small (6 pts) but deliberate.

### C6 (democratic depth) as the most novel criterion

C6 rewards TYPE of democratic engagement, not presence or scale. A concept-stage project that has genuinely designed for community empowerment (giving communities binding decision rights, governance ownership, or real contestability) scores higher on C6 than a widely deployed project that only informs users of decisions made about them. This inverts the v2 logic (which rewarded deployment scale). The IAP2 spectrum is explicit: informs (0–4), consults/involves (5–9), collaborates/empowers (10–12). This criterion will produce some surprising results — large, well-deployed "transparency" platforms may score lower than small, experimental co-governance tools.

### Contradictions reviewed

**C1 vs M4 (power asymmetry boost vs. surveillance-without-governance reduce):** A project building new government digital infrastructure could simultaneously trigger M1 (correcting a power asymmetry) and M4 (increasing surveillance without governance). Both modifiers apply. If the governance layer is tokenistic, M4 dominates. If contestability is real, M1 is stronger. Apply both, note tension in rationale.

**C6 (democratic depth) and C3 (participatory lifecycle):** These can diverge. A project may have deep lifecycle engagement (scoring well on C3) while operating at "informs" democratic depth (scoring low on C6) — e.g., a project that conducts extensive research about communities but ultimately produces information tools that inform rather than empower. This divergence is intentional: C3 asks about process; C6 asks about type of democratic outcome.

**C4 (critical self-examination) and C5 (structural underdog):** A structurally disadvantaged project (scoring high on C5 as grassroots/underfunded) may lack the capacity to produce documented critical self-reflection (scoring low on C4). The partial suspension of C4 at low completeness (floor 4 pts) mitigates this. The traceability requirement further ensures that lack of documentation is distinguished from lack of reflexivity.

**M2 (reduce empowerment-as-profiling) and C2/C3 (centring excluded communities, participatory lifecycle):** A project serving excluded communities with genuine co-design could still trigger M2 if it also expands institutional profiling capacity without community control. Resolution: M2 is triggered by absence of genuine community control over data use, not data collection per se. A project where the community controls the data is not subject to M2.

### Gaps identified

**Gap 1:** No dedicated criterion for epistemic justice / information integrity tools. These score on C4 (critical engagement) and C1 (epistemic power asymmetries via M1), but not strongly on C2 unless there is a specific excluded-community dimension. The SKEPSIS background suggests she would value information integrity work, but the constitution currently underweights it. M7 (intra-class dynamics) and M1 (epistemic redress) partially address this.

**Gap 2:** No dedicated criterion for civic deliberation or participatory democracy infrastructure. These score on C6 (democratic depth — "collaborates"/"empowers" end), C3 (participatory lifecycle), and C1 (redistributing decision-making), but there is no criterion that directly rewards the quality of deliberative design. This may systematically undervalue deliberative democracy work relative to direct service contestability tools.

**Gap 3:** The class inequalities framing (central to her feedback) is reflected in C2 high-scoring descriptors and M7, but the constitution as a whole does not have a criterion that directly rewards analysis of who benefits from exclusion (not just who is excluded). Projects that examine landlord interests, institutional complicity, or class dynamics of who profits from exclusion may be undervalued relative to projects that centre excluded communities without that analysis.

### Operational readiness

Scoring normalisation: criteria sum max = 82; divide raw criteria score by 0.82 to normalise to 100-pt ceiling; apply modifiers additively (net adjustment); final score clamped 0–100.

**C6 prototype rule:** eligible prototypes C6 max = 8; theory can substitute for up to 60% of C6 (max 7.2 pts from theory if C6 max is 12; max 4.8 pts from theory if C6 is capped at 8 for prototypes).

Constitution is operationally ready for the ranking agent.
