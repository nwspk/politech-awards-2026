## Winner Comparison

**V2 winner:** CONSUL Democracy (84.3)
**V3 winner:** CONSUL Democracy (84.7)

Same winner, nearly the same score (+0.4). The new weights redistributed points significantly across the longlist, but CONSUL Democracy's broad strength across all criteria — strong accessibility signals, open source with active community governance, documented government legibility, and wide deployment — kept it at the top under both framings. CONSUL is not a product of any single weighting scheme; it performs well because it does many things right.

---

## Key Changes from V2

### C6 tripling (12 → 30 pts): the biggest structural change

The promotion of C6 from MEDIUM (12 pts) to HIGH (30 pts) was the single largest structural change in v3. Projects with strong community infrastructure framing — decentralised, privacy-first, non-extractive — received significant boosts. Projects with surveillance-adjacent features or extractive data practices received stronger reduces than in v2.

The effect was to create a clearer separation between projects that treat technology as community infrastructure and those that do not. In v2, the C6 signal was present but weak (max 12 pts, easily overwhelmed by other criteria). In v3, it is equally weighted with accessibility, open source, and legibility — meaning a project that scores poorly on C6 cannot compensate through other criteria alone.

### C5 reduction (12 → 6 pts): implementation matters less as a criterion

The demotion of C5 means implementation maturity is less of a direct scoring driver. However, the new M_IMPL modifier compensates: well-deployed projects with government adoption at scale receive +6–12 via M_IMPL, partially replacing the lost C5 weight. For the top projects (CONSUL, vTaiwan, LiquidFeedback), the net effect is roughly neutral — they gained M_IMPL boost but lost C5 weight. For mid-table projects with moderate deployment, the net effect is a slight decrease relative to v2.

The projects that benefited most from the C5 demotion are those with strong conceptual/architectural design but limited deployment — Bonfire (#11) is the clearest example, rising from a lower v2 position because its strong C2 and C6 signals are no longer penalised by a high-weight implementation gap.

### vTaiwan enters the top 10 at #7

vTaiwan's rise to v3 #7 is largely explained by M_IMPL. Under implementation-first framing, vTaiwan's government institutional adoption — formal use by the Taiwan Executive Yuan for policy consultation — earns full M_IMPL boost. Its governance legibility score (C3) is also strong because it makes regulatory and policy processes directly navigable by citizens. In v2, vTaiwan was not in the top 10.

### Open Digital Planning enters the top 10 at #8

Open Digital Planning — UK planning software with strong government adoption evidence — benefits from the same dynamics as vTaiwan: documented institutional use, clear government process legibility (C3), and non-extractive data handling. Its rise to #8 in v3 reflects M_IMPL applied to a project with clear government deployment in UK local authorities.

### Mid-table reshuffled by M_IMPL

The most visible effect of M_IMPL on the overall ranking is in the 50–200 rank range. Projects with weak deployment evidence dropped relative to their v2 positions. Open Access – Transparency International rose to #75 (up from lower in v2) because of cross-country deployment evidence. deliberAIde dropped to #60 from higher in v2 — its government partnership evidence is present but limited to specific pilots rather than sustained adoption.

## What Remained Stable

The top 5 positions are stable between v2 and v3: CONSUL Democracy, Polis, LiquidFeedback, Decidim, and Open Data Editor (ODE) all appear in both top 10s. These projects earn their positions across multiple criteria simultaneously and are not sensitive to any single weight change. This suggests the v2 constitution was broadly well-calibrated for identifying the strongest projects — the v3 changes refined the ranking without overturning it.

---

## Inference About the Evaluator

The stability of the top result despite significant weight changes suggests that Fatima's "primitives over platforms, agency over participation" feedback is pointing toward a different framing question rather than a different winner. CONSUL Democracy is, in the evidence available, both well-deployed AND community-governed — it satisfies both the implementation-first and agency-first lenses. The real divergence between v3 and v5 is in the projects that satisfy one lens but not both. That analysis belongs in the v4 synthesis.
