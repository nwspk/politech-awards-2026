### Criterion 1: Accessibility of civic and political technology for excluded communities
- **Weight:** HIGH (max 30 pts)
- **Why Fatima:** Her entire career arc — from building a regulatory compliance app for Canadian drone pilots (Code for Canada × Transport Canada), to her Sessionize bio foregrounding "simplifying technical concepts" and "keynotes on inclusion & allyship," to her Medium bio's explicit pairing of "open source x intersectionality" — consistently prioritises making complex systems legible and accessible to people who are currently excluded from them. [Sessionize, GitHub drone-companion-app, Medium bio — all CONFIRMED]
- **Implementation-first lens (v3):** Accessibility is measured by demonstrated reach. Projects that have actually reduced barriers for real excluded users score higher than those claiming accessibility intent without deployment evidence.
- **High score (80–100):** Projects that demonstrably reduce a specific access barrier — language, literacy, disability, complexity of government bureaucracy — for people who currently cannot participate in civic or political life. Evidence of real users from excluded groups. Projects that make government regulatory processes legible to ordinary citizens.
- **Low score (0–30):** Projects aimed primarily at already-engaged, digitally literate professionals or activists who have multiple alternatives.
- **Dossier fields:** communities_served, primary_users_or_beneficiaries, accessibility_features (if present), geographic_focus, tagline, scraped_description

### Criterion 2: Open source commitment and community governance
- **Weight:** HIGH (max 30 pts)
- **Why Fatima:** Open source is not merely a technical preference — her Medium bio explicitly frames it as "open source x intersectionality," treating it as an ethical and political commitment. [Medium bio, GitHub profile, GitLab co-create post — all CONFIRMED]
- **Implementation-first lens (v3):** Open source with an active, maintained codebase and recent commits carries more weight than dormant repositories. Community governance mechanisms must be operational, not merely documented.
- **High score (80–100):** Projects that are genuinely open source (code publicly accessible, actively maintained), AND have structured community governance. Active GitHub repositories with recent commits. Community has genuine input into project direction.

### Criterion 3: Making government and civic processes legible and navigable
- **Weight:** HIGH (max 30 pts)
- **Why Fatima:** The drone companion app translates Transport Canada's complex drone regulations into an accessible tool for ordinary citizens. Code for Canada's mission — embedding digital professionals in government to improve public service delivery — is the professional context she chose. [Sessionize bio, GitHub drone-companion-app, Code for Canada fellowship — CONFIRMED]
- **Implementation-first lens (v3):** Projects score higher when they have documented evidence that citizens have actually navigated a previously opaque process more successfully. Government partnerships or formal adoption validate the legibility goal.

- **Dossier fields:** political_relevance_summary, primary_users_or_beneficiaries, issue_area, scraped_description, government_partnerships

### Criterion 6: AI and technology as community infrastructure, not surveillance infrastructure
- **Weight:** HIGH (max 30 pts) — promoted from MEDIUM (12) in v2
- **Why promoted in v3:** Under the implementation-first framing, how technology is deployed at scale — and for whose benefit — is central. Projects that have proven they can deploy technology in ways that empower communities are exactly what implementation-first Fatima would back. [GitLab ChatGPT post, co-create post, Medium bio BIFFUD affiliation — CONFIRMED]
- **Implementation-first lens (v3):** Privacy tools and anti-surveillance infrastructure deployed at scale carry maximum weight. Community-governed platforms with large userbases. Projects that have proven the community-infrastructure model works in practice, not just in theory.
- **High score (80–100):** Projects where AI or technology increases community capacity. Privacy-preserving tools with real users. Federated/decentralised infrastructure under community governance. Tools that fight surveillance rather than enable it.
- **Low score (0–30):** Projects that use AI/data to increase state surveillance. Extractive platforms. Tools that automate government processes in ways that reduce human or community oversight.
- **Dossier fields:** ai_involvement, data_practices, political_relevance_summary, documented_limitations, scraped_description
