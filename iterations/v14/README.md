## Heuristic

Projects ranked by standard deviation of scores ascending, restricted to full 17/17 coverage. Low stdev = the committee broadly agrees on how to value this project regardless of constitution. This is not a ranking by quality — it is a ranking by agreement.

## Rationale

Low variance has two very different meanings. It can mean a project is universally respected — a cross-constitution signal that something is genuinely strong. Or it can mean a project is universally seen as fine but unremarkable — too modest, too narrow, or too legible to any constitution to generate strong views either way. Separating these two readings is the analytical work this list invites.

## Data sources

- `iterations/project-mirror-v2/committee-aggregation/consensus-projects.csv`
- 17 member constitution score sets (14×v2 + 3×v3) from v11

## Limitations

- Requires 17/17 coverage — projects ranked by fewer members are excluded
- Low stdev says nothing about score level — consensus can be consensus on mediocrity
- Constitutional diversity means "agreement" may reflect shared blind spots as much as genuine convergence

## Assessment

The most agreed-upon project is Vote for Policies (stdev 7.59, mean 32.54), but the agreement is that it is limited: discontinued after the 2024 election, narrowly UK-focused, treating voters as passive consumers. The consensus is quiet convergence on a modest ceiling, not enthusiasm.

The more interesting cases sit further down the list. mySociety Datasets and APIs (stdev 16.40, mean 61.11) achieves broad agreement on quality rather than shared indifference — researchers, FOI influence, institutional partnerships, and open APIs map onto enough different constitutions that convergence is possible. Civic Tech Field Guide (stdev 7.93, mean 42.63) sits near the top of consensus but the agreement reflects a shared view that it is valuable but not transformative, with multiple members independently flagging its Western/English-language bias as the limiting factor.
