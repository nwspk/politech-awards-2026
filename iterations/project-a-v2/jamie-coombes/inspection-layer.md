## Summary Note

This inspection layer reveals a constitution with a clear primary winner at the jury level (AlgorithmWatch, 33% of all votes) but genuine ambiguity in the primary scoring (ranked 8th). The gap between scoring rank 8 and jury plurality is the central diagnostic: the constitution correctly identifies that no project on the shortlist satisfies mechanistic interpretability (C1) at high weight, which means the top 10 is dominated by civic infrastructure tools. When the jury applies the full constitution holistically, it gravitates toward AlgorithmWatch because it best combines AI accountability (C1 partial), government applicability (C2), and systemic risk (C3) despite not maximising any of them. The constitution's failure mode — over-weighting government legibility of civic infrastructure — is real and documented. If Jamie Coombes reads this ranking, the most useful question is whether he would endorse mySociety Datasets and APIs over AlgorithmWatch, or vice versa, and what that reveals about whether C2 (government applicability) means "used by government" or "makes government AI accountable."
