## What Prism Would Champion

The clearest winners under Prism's constitution are projects that do what she does professionally: take complex, often counterintuitive data and make it legible to general audiences in ways that challenge distorted public perceptions. The constitutional archetype is Gapminder Worldview Upgrader — a project explicitly designed to quantify the gap between what people believe about global health, poverty, and development and what the evidence actually shows. It hits every high-weight criterion simultaneously: it is built for public evidence-legibility, it corrects perception gaps with primary data, its methodology is transparent, and its counter-narrative function is the point of the project rather than a side effect. At the same tier, projects that combine open-data infrastructure with methodological honesty score strongly — Open Data Editor, ODK, CKAN, and mySociety's datasets and APIs all reward the evaluator who asks not just "what does this do?" but "how does it do it and can I verify that?" The constitution also reserves meaningful credit for counter-narrative journalism infrastructure: Bellingcat's investigation toolkit, Atlas of Surveillance, and DISARM Frameworks score well because they are systems for systematically documenting what institutions deny or obscure — precisely the function Prism's work at OWID performs for migration and health data. The connecting thread is that these projects treat rigorous evidence not as a constraint but as the argument itself.

---

## What Prism Would Discount

The constitution is structurally unable to see the value in projects that do genuinely important civic work through means it doesn't reward. Participatory democracy platforms — Decidim, Consul, DemocracyOS, Citizen OS — score in the 60s and 70s under this constitution, but the jury models (which recognise their reputations) rank them far higher. The reason is specific: these platforms enable participation and deliberation, but the constitution cares primarily about evidence legibility and methodological transparency, and a participatory platform's value lies in the quality of the conversation it enables rather than in making data transparent or correcting perception gaps. Similarly, civic organising and movement infrastructure tools (Activist Handbook, Organizing tools, coalition-building platforms) score modestly because the constitution's structural solutions criterion rewards institutional reform but doesn't have a strong signal for the kind of community power-building that precedes institutional change. Projects with aspirational language and limited documented metrics — a wide category in civic tech — face the M5 reduction modifier, which the evidence strongly justifies but which may penalise genuinely early-stage work that hasn't yet had the time to accumulate evidence.

---

## Constitutional Failure Mode

The constitution cannot see the value of projects whose excellence lies in the quality of human process they enable rather than in the quality of evidence they produce. Prism's evaluative identity was built entirely from data journalism — a domain where the output is always an evidential claim about the world, and where the key question is always "how do you know?" The criteria are calibrated to assess evidence. But some of the most important political technology projects don't primarily produce evidence — they produce *conditions* for better deliberation, organisation, or solidarity. A tool that helps a marginalised community develop collective voice is not primarily making an evidential claim; it is enabling a process. The constitution's Criterion 4 (structural solutions) is meant to catch some of this, but it still asks about institutional reform rather than community empowerment. Projects doing the hardest civic work in the most resource-constrained contexts will systematically score modestly under a constitution built for an OWID data scientist, not because those projects are weak but because the constitution's lens — evidence legibility, methodological transparency, perception-gap quantification — is measuring a different kind of excellence than what those projects are optimised for.
