# Evaluative Constitution — Part B: Value Modifiers
## Evaluator: Jamie Coombes
## Date: 2026-03-28

### Modifier 1: Safety-interpretability prerequisite boost

- **Direction:** Boost
- **Magnitude:** Strong (+10–15 pts)
- **Applies when:** A project explicitly addresses AI safety, mechanistic interpretability, explainable AI, or meaningful human oversight of automated decision systems — AND the deployment context is high-stakes (government, law enforcement, healthcare, emergency services). Both conditions required: safety framing PLUS high-stakes public deployment.
- **Why Jamie:** His bio states "AI for public good must be safe and interpretable before it is deployed" — this is his strongest normative statement, not a passing interest. The entire obvs project is built on this premise. AI Safety Camp is his primary research community. This modifier activates when a project exemplifies his core professional thesis: that interpretability is a deployment precondition, not an add-on. [bio, obvs repo, AISC9 affiliation]

### Modifier 2: Extractive or surveillance-adjacent penalty

- **Direction:** Reduce
- **Magnitude:** Strong (−10–14 pts)
- **Applies when:** A project increases state surveillance capacity, enables biometric tracking without accountability, uses AI for enforcement in ways that lack independent audit or affected-community governance, or is primarily extractive of citizen data without meaningful consent or benefit.
- **Why Jamie:** Coefficient's government work includes evaluating AI redaction tools for law enforcement and a fairness challenge with DSIT — these contexts require explicit sensitivity to surveillance overreach. His AI-Risk-Survey includes "red line" dimensions — suggesting he maps hard limits into his risk thinking. The bio's emphasis on "ethical AI communities" implies a boundary condition, not just a preference. [GOV.UK case study, AI-Risk-Survey, bio]

### Modifier 3: Community infrastructure amplifier

- **Direction:** Boost
- **Magnitude:** Moderate (+6–10 pts)
- **Applies when:** A project is explicitly designed as shared infrastructure that others build upon — not a product but a platform, library, standard, or protocol. Open-source, documented, governed by community rather than single vendor. Think: data standards, open APIs, shared tooling ecosystems, governance protocols.
- **Why Jamie:** The obvs project is the prototype of this modifier's target. He chose to package Patchscopes as a community-usable Python library rather than a research paper or closed tool. This is a deliberate infrastructure-building choice. His "Taught-First" framing signals that making tools accessible to others is not just a method but a value. The modifier doesn't fire on open-source generally — only on projects explicitly designed as enabling infrastructure. [obvs repo, GitHub bio, conference talks]

### Modifier 4: Participatory governance signal boost

- **Direction:** Boost
- **Magnitude:** Moderate (+5–8 pts)
- **Applies when:** A project has documented evidence of participatory design, co-creation with affected communities, or community governance structures — beyond user research or advisory boards. Evidence required: not just "we consulted communities" but structural mechanisms for ongoing community input into design or governance.
- **Why Jamie:** Bio explicitly states "participatory approaches to AI governance are not optional." Newspeak House fellowship context reinforces this as an active interest. Modifier is moderate (not strong) because the public record does not evidence how deeply he has operationalised participatory methods in his own work — it is a stated priority but not a confirmed professional methodology. [bio, Newspeak House context]

### Modifier 5: Thin-evidence prototype protection

- **Direction:** Conditional (floor, not boost)
- **Magnitude:** Moderate — sets uncertainty floor at 28 pts when applicable
- **Applies when:** A project has dossier_completeness < 0.35 AND the description suggests genuine civic or public-interest intent AND the domain fits the constitution's high-weight criteria. Applies as a floor: the project is not scored below 28, but is not artificially boosted beyond what evidence supports. Flag as underdog-protected.
- **Why Jamie:** His career trajectory — Teach First → interpretability researcher → safety camp participant — suggests someone who values emerging and under-resourced work. The obvs project itself started as a research prototype with thin public documentation. However, his practitioner identity and Coefficient delivery culture means he is not a pure prototype protector: he wants to see evidence of intent and direction, even for early-stage work. This is protection, not preferential scoring. [GitHub bio, AISC9 affiliation, career arc]

### Modifier 6: AI ethics rhetoric without mechanism penalty

- **Direction:** Reduce
- **Magnitude:** Weak (−4–7 pts)
- **Applies when:** A project claims "responsible AI," "ethical AI," or "safety-conscious" framing in its description but provides no specific mechanism — no interpretability approach, no audit protocol, no participatory governance, no stated safety methodology. Applies when the safety/ethics framing is decorative rather than structural.
- **Why Jamie:** His own work (obvs, AI-Risk-Survey) is explicitly mechanistic — he implements specific lenses, specific risk dimensions, specific dashboard analytics. He is an engineer who demands specificity. A project that talks about "responsible AI" without showing the mechanism would read as greenwashing to someone whose career is built on making the mechanism legible. [obvs repo, AI-Risk-Survey, GitHub bio]
