### What we inferred (PROBABLE / WEAK)

- **PROBABLE: The move to Lucid Computing represents a deliberate transition from policy advocacy to technical infrastructure for enforcement.** Lucid Computing builds cryptographic proof of compliance (TEE-based verification, automated audit trails, AI Passports). Given Dunlop's consistent advocacy for enforceable governance and his critique of voluntary approaches, this move appears to be a natural extension of his policy positions into technical implementation. The inference is strong — the career move is coherent with every stated position — but there is no direct statement from Dunlop about why he made the transition.
- **PROBABLE: Dunlop believes the primary bottleneck in AI governance is verification infrastructure, not policy design.** He has spent years arguing for enforcement mechanisms and has now moved to a company that builds exactly those mechanisms. The inference is that he sees the policy framework as relatively established (EU AI Act exists) and the implementation gap as the critical frontier. But this could equally reflect a personal career decision unrelated to his assessment of where the bottleneck lies.
- **WEAK: Dunlop's conflict studies background shapes his approach to AI governance through a human rights and power-analysis lens.** His MA is in conflict studies and human rights, and his policy positions consistently emphasise power asymmetries (information asymmetries between regulators and industry, burden of proof on developers, rights for affected persons). But the causal link between the degree and the positions is inferred — many AI governance practitioners hold similar positions without conflict studies backgrounds.
- **WEAK: Dunlop's private views on compute governance may be more radical than his institutional positions suggest.** Institutional publications (Ada Lovelace, TechPolicy.Press) are consensus-oriented by nature. The move to a startup building hard enforcement infrastructure may indicate a willingness to go further than institutional positions allowed. But this is speculative.
