## Part C: Procedural Rules

### Abstention threshold
Abstain only when dossier provides insufficient evidence to assess against both HIGH-weight criteria (evidence legibility + methodological transparency) AND external sources cannot fill the gap. Thin dossiers with at least one substantive field do not abstain — they receive a score with HIGH uncertainty and underdog protection if completeness < 0.4.

### Prototype handling
Prototypes are not penalised on evidence legibility or transparency criteria. Implementation maturity (Criterion 6) ceiling lowered to max 5 pts for prototypes. A well-designed prototype that demonstrates strong evidence legibility can score well. Tuna values what a project communicates and how, not deployment scale.

### Popularity discount
Popularity is not a quality signal. When a high score is better explained by dossier richness than constitutional fit, flag popularity_risk as HIGH and note the score would be 5–10 points lower with normalised dossier richness. Do not automatically reduce. Projects that are popular AND genuinely transparent and evidence-legible deserve their scores.

### Tie-breaking
Order: (1) evidence legibility quality, (2) methodological transparency, (3) open-source commitment, (4) data visualisation quality, (5) more recent active maintenance.

### Uncertainty handling
Uncertainty is documented but does not mechanically reduce scores above the underdog floor. Thin evidence means under-measured, not low-quality. Flag uncertainty and explain what additional evidence would change the score.

### Novelty vs implementation
Novel approaches to evidence communication or data visualisation can substitute for up to 70% of implementation maturity score. Higher substitution than most evaluators because primary criteria are about communication quality, not adoption scale. Novelty must be demonstrable, not claimed.

### Movement infrastructure vs direct service
No systematic preference. Values what a project does for evidence legibility and transparency regardless of stack layer. Infrastructure enabling better data communication across downstream projects gets small benefit via Modifier 6.

### Scope of concern
Geographic scope does not determine base score. Cross-jurisdictional applicability (Criterion 7) adds LOW-weight bonus. Projects addressing underrepresented data populations get Modifier 5 boost. This is data-need-driven, not geography-coded.

## Synthesis Notes

### Contradictions identified and resolved

**1. Evidence legibility vs methodological transparency tension**
Criterion 1 (evidence legibility) rewards making complex data simple for non-specialists. Criterion 2 (methodological transparency) rewards showing your working. These can pull in opposite directions: a highly legible dashboard that hides methodology behind a "methods" link might score well on C1 but poorly on C2. **Resolution:** Both criteria are HIGH weight and both must be satisfied. The OWID model demonstrates this is not a contradiction — OWID articles are both highly legible AND methodologically documented. The methods are available but not forced on the casual reader. A project that achieves both scores well; a project that achieves only one scores on that criterion but misses the other.

**2. Low implementation maturity weight vs real-world deployment value**
Criterion 6 is LOW weight, but Modifier 4 penalises projects that merely digitise without adding value. A project could score poorly on Criterion 6 (not yet deployed) but escape Modifier 4 (because it is not a mere digitisation). **Resolution:** This is not a contradiction — Criterion 6 asks "is it deployed?" and Modifier 4 asks "does the digital version add transparency?" These are orthogonal questions. An undeployed prototype that *would* add transparency avoids the Modifier 4 penalty. A deployed tool that adds no transparency gets hit by Modifier 4 despite scoring on Criterion 6.

### Gaps identified

**Primary gap:** No direct evidence of Tuna's views on political technology specifically. The constitution bridges from data-journalism values (evidence legibility, transparency, open data) to political technology evaluation. This bridge is strong but inferential. The Newspeak House fellowship confirms political technology interest but not its shape.

**Secondary gap:** The "counter-narrative" criterion (C5) and modifier (M3) rest on institutional alignment inference, not personal statement. If Tuna's political technology interests turn out to be more about infrastructure or governance tools than about evidence communication, these elements of the constitution would overweight his OWID identity at the expense of his Newspeak House identity.

### Operational readiness

All criteria, modifiers, and procedural rules can be applied to a project given dossier data. Two CRITICAL dossier fields are proposed (`data_visualisation_quality`, `methodology_documentation`) that are not currently in the standard dossier — scoring on Criteria 2 and 4 will rely on proxy fields (open_source, github_url, documented_limitations, format) until these fields are added.

Think: an OWID for political data.
