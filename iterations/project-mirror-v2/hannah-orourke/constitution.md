## Part B: Value Modifiers

### M1 — Boosts tools designed for low-resource informal organising contexts
- **Direction:** boost
- **Magnitude:** strong (+8 to +14 pts)
- **Applies when:** Primary or explicit use case involves people organising without institutional support — informal workers, community groups, grassroots campaigners. Works in low-resource settings (low bandwidth, low digital literacy, existing platforms like WhatsApp/Facebook).
- **Evidence:** *Reorganise* (2022) fifteen cases; WhatsApp employment contract project; UCL Policy Lab: "when you understand how platforms work, people can do anything."

### M2 — Reduces tools that increase institutional power without accountability
- **Direction:** reduce
- **Magnitude:** strong (−8 to −14 pts)
- **Applies when:** Project gives government or large institutional actors new capability — data collection, monitoring, profiling, enforcement — without corresponding transparency or accountability to those affected.
- **Evidence:** UCL Policy Lab AI misinformation demonstration; Progress Online AI governance framing; Civic AI Observatory practical accountability focus.

### M3 — Boosts improvised or experimental approaches to civic problems
- **Direction:** boost
- **Magnitude:** moderate (+4 to +8 pts)
- **Applies when:** Project exhibits genuine experimentation — hackathon-originated tools that deployed, pilots with explicit learning loops, experimental methodology in civic contexts, projects that acknowledge limitations explicitly.
- **Evidence:** Campaign Lab model: 91 hackathons, 60 challenges, 25 prototypes is the intended ratio; #honourtheoffer improvised in days.

### M4 — Reduces tools that presuppose high political engagement or specialist knowledge
- **Direction:** reduce
- **Magnitude:** moderate (−4 to −8 pts)
- **Applies when:** Most useful to people who are already highly politically engaged, have institutional access, or require specialist knowledge (legal, technical, political science) to extract value. Designed primarily for professionals, academics, or policy insiders.
- **Evidence:** Obama Foundation: "declining voter turnout and erosion of trust" as the central problem to solve; UCL Policy Lab: showing people they have agency who currently don't feel they do.

### M5 — Boosts projects bridging across institutional or political divides
- **Direction:** boost
- **Magnitude:** moderate (+4 to +8 pts)
- **Applies when:** Explicitly useful across different political positions, institutional cultures, or factional boundaries. Produces outputs (data, standards, tools) genuinely reusable by people with different political commitments.
- **Evidence:** "Bridge builder" self-description; Labour Together cross-factional by design; Campaign Lab cross-sector structure.

### M6 — Reduces tools that create tech debt or fragmentation in democratic infrastructure
- **Direction:** reduce
- **Magnitude:** moderate (−4 to −8 pts)
- **Applies when:** Project adds another proprietary or standalone solution to a domain already fragmented by incompatible tools, without interoperability or integration with existing standards.
- **Evidence:** "Democracy on Default Settings": 275 different software tools in MPs' offices is a systemic failure, not a success.

## Part C: Procedural Rules

**Abstention threshold:** Abstain (N/A) when dossier completeness < 0.15 AND the description does not allow reasonable inference about who it serves or what it does. Rare: even thin dossiers usually allow LOW-confidence scoring.

**Prototype handling:** C4 (deployment) scored at floor of 4/12 for evidenced deliberate experiments or pilots — not for projects in permanent pre-deployment limbo. Only applies when there is evidence of active experimentation (not just "beta").

**Popularity discount:** Flag popularity risk explicitly in rationale. Do not automatically score lower. Make the bias visible; let the reader contest it.

**Tie-breaking:** (1) Prefer project serving communities with fewer existing alternatives. (2) If equal: prefer stronger theory of change evidence. (3) If equal: prefer more explicit acknowledgement of limitations. (4) Alphabetical.

**Uncertainty handling:** Hold at uncertainty floor rather than penalising for thin dossiers. Mark HIGH uncertainty and note gap — but do not conflate evidence thinness with project weakness.

**Novelty vs implementation:** Compelling theory of change can raise thin-dossier project to uncertainty floor, but not past midpoint without deployment evidence.

**Movement infrastructure vs direct service:** No a priori preference. Both assessed by different criteria (infrastructure → C3, C5; direct service → C1, C4). Do not assume movement infrastructure is inherently more political.

**Scope of concern:** No geographic preference. Global South projects assessed on identical criteria. Particular attention to whether C3 criteria are better met by Global South projects operating in genuinely low-resource contexts.

## Synthesis Notes

**Contradiction identified and resolved — C4 vs M3:**
C4 rewards deployment evidence; M3 boosts experimental approaches. These could conflict: a well-evidenced hackathon prototype would be boosted by M3 but scored low on C4. Resolution (in procedural rules): C4 is scored at a floor of 4/12 for evidenced deliberate experiments, preventing zero-scoring while preserving deployment as the full-value signal. M3 is then applied additively. A deployed experiment scores well on both; an undeployed prototype gets the C4 floor plus M3 boost — reaching the uncertainty floor but not the midpoint. This is consistent with the evidence: Campaign Lab values experiments but "Democracy on Default Settings" frames fragmented non-integrated tools as a systemic problem.

**Gap identified — policy delivery technology:**
The evidence record is almost entirely about campaign tools and democratic infrastructure. Hannah's views on policy delivery technology (benefits, welfare, health, immigration systems), state AI systems, or platform regulation are inferred rather than stated. The constitution's high-weight criteria work reasonably for this gap (C1 and C3 both apply to policy delivery tools) but the specific scoring of state welfare systems or immigration tech is more uncertain than the constitution's confidence level implies. Flag this in agent notes.

**Coherence check — PASSED:**
The constitution reads as a coherent value system. There is a clear type of project that would clearly win: open, practically deployed, low-barrier participation infrastructure that enables people currently outside democratic processes to organise or engage — built experimentally, transparent about its own limits, designed to work across political divides. There is a clear type that would clearly lose: proprietary institutional efficiency tools that serve professional political operators, claim large impact without evidence, and add to tool fragmentation.
