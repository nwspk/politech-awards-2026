## Constitutional Failure Mode

The built-in blind spot of this constitution is its inability to see the democratic value of intermediary expertise. Hannah's constitution is oriented entirely toward reducing the gap between citizens and civic power — tools that lower activation thresholds, that work for people without institutional resources, that make participation accessible to the disengaged. This is a coherent and evidence-based value. But it means the constitution systematically undervalues a class of tools whose democratic value operates through expert intermediaries rather than directly: investigative journalism tools, legislative monitoring systems for policy researchers, legal aid platforms requiring qualified practitioners, and complex data infrastructure that produces the evidence base that democratic accountability depends on. These tools do democratic work — they enable the journalism that changes elections, the legal challenges that change law, the evidence that changes policy — but they do it through professionals, not through the disengaged citizen who is the constitution's primary concern. A project like WhatDoTheyKnow or Aleph scores reasonably because transparency and accountability keywords help, but a pure data infrastructure tool serving only research journalists would score poorly — even if it is the foundation of democratic accountability in its context. The constitution cannot see value that does not flow directly through the citizen it wants to empower.
