## Evidence Summary

Hannah O'Rourke's public record is thinner than average but the sources that exist are high-signal. She is co-founder of two significant institutions: Campaign Lab, a UK civic tech hackathon network focused on evidence-based campaigning, and Labour Together, the cross-factional Labour organisation that commissioned the major 2019 Labour Election Review.

The clearest signal about her values emerges from *Reorganise* (2022), a Autonomy think-tank paper she co-authored examining 15 case studies of digital labour organising in informal or low-resource settings — WhatsApp groups for employment contract reviews, gig economy worker coordination tools, community self-organisation without traditional union structures. This paper is the most substantive primary source and establishes her as a practitioner deeply interested in how platforms enable organising by people who lack institutional resources.

Her UCL Policy Lab affiliation adds a research and academic dimension, with documented interest in when technology is and is not the right solution ("sometimes people jump straight to tech, when the best solution might be something like a really well-written guide"). This is an epistemically distinctive position — she evaluates technology against non-technological alternatives.

*Democracy on Default Settings* (2026) is the most recent piece and the most directly relevant to the award themes: it diagnoses MP office technology as fragmented (275 different software tools), structurally dysfunctional, and overwhelmingly outsourced to private vendors without coordination, resulting in MPs unable to serve constituents effectively. This is a systemic infrastructure critique, not a tool recommendation.

The Obama Foundation digital organising work is less documented but consistent: she runs programmes serving "thousands of users across constituencies" focused on building civic participation capacity for people experiencing declining trust and engagement.
