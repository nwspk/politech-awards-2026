## Source 2: Blog post — "Why I Mistrust the Term 'Tech for Good'" (22 Feb 2026)
- **Type:** blog
- **Year:** 2026
- **URL:** https://www.davidbuildstech.com/why-i-mistrust-the-term-tech-for-good/
- **Confidence:** HIGH — first-person, self-authored, substantial

### Content:
- Argues companies labelling themselves "Tech for Good" often fail to align practices with stated missions
- Structural incentives of capitalism lead to exploitation of workers and beneficiaries
- Examples given:
  - "John" worked on a government partnership project funded by an NGO where actual government users never adopted the platform; company's open-source release was "more of a PR exercise than a genuine attempt"
  - "Tim" took pay cut for equity at educational inequality startup; found wage disparities including a person of colour paid substantially less despite equality mission
- Core argument: "Tech for Good" companies offer below-market salaries using meaningful work as compensation, attracting idealistic talent while exploiting them
- **Proposed structural questions to assess organisations:**
  1. Legal structure (for-profit vs non-profit vs cooperative)
  2. Founder profit potential and exponential return expectations
  3. Funding methodology alignment
  4. Decision-making power distribution and oversight
  5. Organisational transparency regarding finances, salaries, decisions
- **Positive examples cited:**
  - Ghost — non-profit platform, open-source, cloud-funded, plans to cap at 50 employees
  - Poteris — non-profit with flat pay structure, 4-day weeks, progressive hourly rates
  - Outlandish — technology cooperative
  - Common Knowledge — technology cooperative
- Conclusion: good intentions insufficient; structural changes necessary for genuine public-interest technology

### Analytical notes:
This is the single most valuable source for constitutional inference. It directly states David's evaluative framework: **structure over intention**. He judges organisations not by what they say they do but by how they are legally structured, funded, governed, and how power is distributed. This is a first-person, recent, detailed articulation of values.
