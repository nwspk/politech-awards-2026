## Evidence summary

**Paragraph 1 -- The activist.** Davit Jintcharadze's public identity is dominated by his role in the Georgian pro-democracy movement. He co-founded the Freedom Square party and separately founded the Freedom Fund, a UK-based crowdfunding vehicle that has raised over GBP 42,000 to support protesters. He organised an international awareness campaign (the Timberlake petition, covered by OCCRP) and published a policy op-ed at CEPA arguing for targeted judicial sanctions and defector-protection programs to weaken the Georgian Dream regime. His direct quote -- "The main goal is to tell the world about what is happening in Georgia" -- captures his outward-facing strategy: leveraging international attention as a pressure mechanism against authoritarianism.

**Paragraph 2 -- The psychologist.** His academic training is in psychology (BA NYU Abu Dhabi, pursuing psychotherapy qualification at Cambridge). He has one peer-reviewed publication in Scientific Reports (2023) on how facial masculinity/femininity affects competence judgments of politicians and election outcome prediction -- directly relevant to voter psychology. His Substack, "Politicising Psychology," frames protest through psychoanalytic theory (Winnicott, art therapy). The bio's claim about "voter psychology education campaigns" is not independently verified through a specific campaign name or external source, but the combination of his academic research, his Substack framing, and his party role makes it plausible.

**Paragraph 3 -- The EA community builder.** Jintcharadze has deep roots in the effective altruism community. He founded EA Georgia (October 2022), launched the Opportunity Center with CEA funding to help Georgian students access global universities and work on pressing issues (AI safety, mental health), and works as Operations Manager at The Unjournal (an EA-aligned research evaluation non-profit). His EA Forum post on making EA relevant to minority groups reveals a clear tension he navigates: he believes EA's Western-centric framing alienates people in conflict-affected countries, and he advocates connecting global risks (e.g., AI surveillance of sexual orientation) to the lived experiences of marginalised communities.

**Paragraph 4 -- The biographical arc.** His trajectory runs from Georgian high school student (FLEX exchange, California, 2016) through NYU Abu Dhabi (full scholarship, psychology major, student journalism, refugee storybook illustration) to Cambridge and London. He moved from humanitarian volunteering (refugee storybook, Syrian refugee letter-writing) to political organising (Freedom Square, Freedom Fund) as Georgia's democratic situation deteriorated from 2024 onward. His current positioning straddles three worlds: professional EA operations work (The Unjournal), academic psychotherapy training (Cambridge), and frontline political activism (Georgian democracy movement).

**Paragraph 5 -- What is clearest and what is ambiguous.** The clearest signal about his values: democratic resilience is existentially urgent, disinformation and psychological manipulation are real weapons, and technology must serve people in conflict-affected and LMIC contexts -- not just wealthy democracies. He is deeply sceptical of Western-centric framings of global problems. The ambiguity: the bio claims he "co-founded" Freedom Square, but no English-language press source lists him as a founding member (the Wikipedia and Civil Georgia articles list different names). The CEPA bio calls him "one of the founding members," which may reflect a difference between the public-facing leadership team and a broader founding circle. The bio's claim about "190 days of protests" aligns with the broader 2024-2026 Georgian protest movement timeline but cannot be pinned to his personal participation count. The bio's focus on "tech for measuring/countering opinion manipulation" has no corresponding public project, tool, or publication -- this may describe an aspiration or a project not yet public.
