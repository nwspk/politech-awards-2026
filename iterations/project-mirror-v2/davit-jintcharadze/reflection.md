# Reflection -- Davit Jintcharadze

## 1. What Davit Would Champion

- **Open-source accountability tools deployed in backsliding democracies** -- The constitution's heaviest weights (C1: 25pts + C2: 20pts + M1: +15pts) create a structural advantage of up to 60 points for projects operating in contested non-Western contexts. Guardian Project (encrypted communication for activists in hostile states), Ushahidi (crisis mapping born in Kenyan post-election violence), and Martus (human rights documentation under repression) all score in the top 5 because they hit this archetype cleanly.

- **First-of-kind civic infrastructure for populations with no prior alternative** -- M4 (+5-10pts) rewards projects filling absolute gaps rather than improving existing options. This reflects his pattern of founding first-of-kind interventions: EA Georgia, Freedom Fund, the refugee storybook. OpenCRVS (civil registration for countries with none) and VFRAME (visual forensics for conflict documentation) score well here.

- **Strategic pressure mechanisms that impose material costs on authoritarian actors** -- His CEPA article is a strategic policy blueprint, not a moral plea. M3 (+5-10pts) rewards projects like Open Supply Hub (supply chain accountability) and sanctions-tracking tools. Documentation alone doesn't earn this boost -- the project must create actionable consequences.

- **Grassroots movement infrastructure over institutional gatekeeping** -- M6 conditionally boosts projects enabling direct community mobilisation (crowdfunding, protest logistics, diaspora support). His Freedom Fund and Change.org petition demonstrate this preference for bottom-up action.

## 2. What Davit Would Discount

- **UK-centric civic convenience tools** -- Labour Xchange, PolicyMogul, WorkInCharities, and similar projects score near the bottom because C1 finds no democratic resilience dimension, C2 finds no LMIC relevance, C3 finds no marginalised population, and C4 finds no international accountability mechanism. The inference is defensible: nothing in his public record suggests he prioritises making stable-democracy governance marginally more efficient.

- **Western-deployed EA-adjacent tools that happen to have no LMIC presence** -- AISafety.info, Understanding Your Morality, and similar projects receive M2 penalties despite potential alignment with his EA commitments. C6 (EA-informed impact reasoning) carries only 10 points and is overwhelmed by the geographic criteria. The discount on stable-democracy work is intentional; the discount on EA-adjacent tools may be an artefact of geographic weighting.

- **Surveillance-adjacent civic tech without community accountability** -- M5 (-10-15pts) penalises projects collecting data about citizens without documented community oversight. His EA Forum post connecting AI safety to LGBTQ+ vulnerability, combined with his personal stakes as an anti-regime activist, grounds this penalty.

- **Projects that claim global applicability without non-Western deployment** -- M2 (-5-10pts) targets false universalism. Projects framing themselves as "global" but tested only in OECD countries trigger this penalty, reflecting his explicit critique of Western-centric framing.

## 3. Constitutional Failure Mode

The constitution's most important blind spot is its inability to value disinformation countermeasures, opinion manipulation research, and psychological resilience tools -- precisely the domain Davit's bio identifies as his core technical interest. His bio states a focus on "tech for measuring/countering opinion manipulation in LMIC countries, disinformation + psychological bias." He has a peer-reviewed publication on political face perception. He studied psychology at Cambridge. Yet the constitution contains no criterion that specifically rewards counter-disinformation technology or psychological manipulation tools.

C5 (evidence-informed approach, 10 pts) rewards methodology but not the subject matter. C1 (democratic resilience, 25 pts) could in principle encompass counter-disinformation work, but its scoring rubric emphasises institutions, civic space, and election integrity -- structural protections, not cognitive ones. A project building tools to detect coordinated inauthentic behaviour on Georgian social media, or developing inoculation-based media literacy interventions grounded in psychological research, would have no dedicated criterion and would depend entirely on the scoring agent's willingness to read C1 broadly. The mechanism of failure is that the constitution was built from Davit's activist and humanitarian record -- which is well-documented -- rather than from his research and technical interests, which are thinner in the public evidence base. The constitution sees Davit the organiser clearly but Davit the psychologist-technologist only dimly.
