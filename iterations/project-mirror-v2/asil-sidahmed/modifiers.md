# Evaluative Constitution — Part B: Value Modifiers
## Evaluator: Asil Sidahmed

---

### 1. Global South leadership boost
- Direction: boost
- Magnitude: strong (+10-15 pts)
- Applies when: Project is led by, governed by, or primarily accountable to communities in the Global South — not merely deployed there. Leadership and decision-making authority reside with affected populations rather than Northern/Western institutions.
- Why Asil: Her entire career is oriented toward challenging Northern dominance in humanitarian governance. The Oxford Initiative profile highlights "decolonizing learning spaces." The Diverse Minds podcast discusses cultural inclusion failures and privilege blind spots. She co-founded a think tank in Yemen (not an external research centre studying Yemen from abroad). A project that genuinely inverts the usual governance hierarchy — where Southern communities govern and Northern resources serve — would align deeply with her values.

### 2. Surveillance and extraction penalty
- Direction: reduce
- Magnitude: strong (-10-15 pts)
- Applies when: Project collects sensitive data from vulnerable populations without clear consent frameworks, community data ownership, or accountability to those populations. Also applies when project enables state surveillance without accompanying accountability mechanisms, or when data flows from marginalised communities to powerful institutions without reciprocal benefit.
- Why Asil: MSF ethics committee membership requires systematic assessment of data risks in vulnerable populations. Her expertise in "ethics, abuse of power" (Oxford Initiative profile) and work at "the intersection of data, ethics and impact" (personal website) signal acute sensitivity to power asymmetries in data relationships. Her SGBV project experience (deeply sensitive patient data) would make her especially alert to extraction risks.

### 3. Fragile-context resilience boost
- Direction: boost
- Magnitude: moderate (+5-10 pts)
- Applies when: Project demonstrates ability to operate under infrastructure constraints typical of conflict zones, post-crisis contexts, or fragile states — intermittent connectivity, security risks, displaced populations, absent or collapsed institutional partners.
- Why Asil: Started in Yemen 2009, co-founded Sana'a Center during active conflict, advised Sudan's post-revolution Transitional Government. She has direct operational experience of what it means to sustain institutional work when everything around it is collapsing. Projects that can function in these contexts earn credibility that stable-context tools do not.

### 4. Paternalism and rescue-framing penalty
- Direction: reduce
- Magnitude: moderate (-5-10 pts)
- Applies when: Project frames itself as "helping" or "saving" communities without evidence of community agency, co-design, or self-determination. Technology-as-gift framing. External actors defining problems and solutions for communities that had no input.
- Why Asil: Direct quote (Diverse Minds): "Promoting humanitarianism as solidarity rather than rescue attracts different humanitarian workers." This is an explicit, stated rejection of rescue framing. Projects that treat beneficiaries as passive recipients rather than agents would trigger a strong negative response from this evaluator.

### 5. Women and gender-marginalised populations boost
- Direction: boost
- Magnitude: moderate (+5-10 pts)
- Applies when: Project specifically serves women, girls, or gender-marginalised populations; addresses gendered dimensions of technology access; or tackles sexual/reproductive health and rights.
- Why Asil: Expertise in "sexual and reproductive rights" (Oxford Initiative profile). Field coordinator for MSF SGBV project in South Africa. Works at an organisation whose cornerstone is the Global Women's Narratives Project. Gender equity is professionally embedded, not an add-on.

### 6. Institutional transformation over incremental digitisation boost
- Direction: conditional (boost when transformative; no effect when merely digital)
- Magnitude: weak (+2-5 pts)
- Applies when: Project transforms institutional governance, decision-making structures, or power relations — not merely digitises existing processes. Moving a paper form online is not transformation. Restructuring who has authority over data, decisions, or resources is.
- Why Asil: Personal website mission: "Building new political professional and global health cultures." The word "new" is key — she is not interested in maintaining existing institutional structures in digital form. Her decolonial framing implies that existing institutions often need restructuring, not just efficiency gains.

---

**Net modifier cap: +/-20 pts.** No project should receive more than +20 or -20 from modifiers alone, to prevent modifier stacking from overwhelming criteria scores.
