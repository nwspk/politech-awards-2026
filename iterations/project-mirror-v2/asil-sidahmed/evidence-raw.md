## Evidence Summary

**Paragraph 1 — Career arc and core identity**
Asil Sidahmed's public record documents a career that spans over 15 years in humanitarian advocacy, global health policy, and conflict-zone strategic advisory. She entered the aid sector in Yemen in 2009, co-founded the Sana'a Center for Strategic Studies (a Yemen-focused think tank) in 2015, and spent a decade at MSF Belgium in progressively senior roles: from advocacy strategist supporting operations across Africa, the Middle East, Europe, and Latin America to Strategic Advisor for Advocacy and ethics committee member at MSF headquarters in Brussels. She served as field coordinator for MSF's sexual and gender-based violence project in South Africa (2019), managing 50+ staff and 4 health clinics. She then served as Strategic Advisor to Sudan's Federal Minister of Health during the Transitional Government (~2021), overseeing health sector strategic planning, maternal mortality policy, and COVID-19 response. She is currently Director of Policy and Health Equity Fellow at the Oxford Initiative for Global Ethics and Human Rights, and a Newspeak House 2025/26 fellowship candidate described as a "governance strategist."

**Paragraph 2 — Values signal from the record**
The clearest and most consistent value signal is a commitment to patient-centred, equity-driven, decolonial approaches to health and humanitarian practice. Her Oxford Initiative profile states advocacy must be "patient-centred, connected to social movements, and carry an inclusive, ethical backbone." In the Diverse Minds podcast, she argues for "promoting humanitarianism as solidarity rather than rescue" — a direct challenge to traditional paternalistic aid models. Her personal website describes her work at "the intersection of data, ethics and impact" and frames her mission as "building new political professional and global health cultures." She teaches on data ethics, institutional transformation through a public health lens, and decolonising learning spaces. This is not a technologist's profile — it is a governance thinker whose concern is the political and ethical infrastructure that surrounds technology, not technology itself.

Asil's connection to political technology is indirect but substantive. She has advocated for blockchain in healthcare contexts during her Sudan advisory role. She teaches on data ethics and institutional transformation. She co-founded a think tank (Sana'a Center) in a conflict zone, demonstrating institutional infrastructure-building capacity. Her MSF work involved coordinating advocacy across 30+ countries, requiring digital infrastructure at scale.
