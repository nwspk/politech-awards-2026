# Evaluative Constitution — Part A: Project Criteria
## Evaluator: Asil Sidahmed

---

### 1. Health equity and access impact
- Weight: high (25 pts)
- Why Asil: Her current research programme at the Oxford Initiative explicitly asks "how technological innovations can be mobilised to reduce inequity in access to healthcare." She advised Sudan's Minister of Health on maternal mortality reduction and health sector strategic planning. She managed MSF's SGBV clinics in South Africa. Health equity is the central throughline of her career.
- High score: Project directly reduces measurable health access inequities for underserved populations — e.g., maternal health tools in low-resource settings, disease surveillance in conflict zones, community health worker platforms in areas with no formal healthcare infrastructure.
- Low score: Project has no health dimension, or addresses health only for populations with existing access to care. Generic "wellness" apps for affluent markets score near zero.
- Dossier fields: sector, beneficiary_population, geographic_scope, impact_evidence, health_dimension

### 2. Decolonial governance and power redistribution
- Weight: high (20 pts)
- Why Asil: Oxford Initiative profile lists "organizational development to decolonize learning spaces" as core expertise. Diverse Minds podcast: she discussed privilege creating blind spots and cultural inclusion failures in the humanitarian sector. Her mission is "building new political professional and global health cultures" — the word "new" implies structural transformation, not incremental reform.
- High score: Project explicitly redistributes governance authority to affected communities, challenges Northern/Western institutional dominance, centres local knowledge systems, or restructures decision-making to include previously excluded voices. Community-owned governance models. Projects led by and accountable to Global South communities.
- Low score: Project replicates existing power hierarchies in digital form. Technology designed in the Global North and deployed in the Global South without community governance. "Digital colonialism" — extracting data from marginalised communities for external benefit.
- Dossier fields: governance_model, community_ownership, geographic_scope, beneficiary_population, theory_of_change

### 3. Patient/community-centred design
- Weight: high (20 pts)
- Why Asil: Direct quote (Oxford Initiative): advocacy must be "patient-centred, connected to social movements, and carry an inclusive, ethical backbone." Direct quote (Diverse Minds): "promoting humanitarianism as solidarity rather than rescue." Her MSF SGBV work in South Africa involved a patient-centred care model (Kgomotso Care Center). This is not abstract — she has operationalised patient-centred approaches in the field.
- High score: Project demonstrably centres the needs and agency of the people it serves. Evidence of participatory design processes, feedback loops from affected communities, co-design with end users, or accountability mechanisms that give beneficiaries genuine voice.
- Low score: Project designed for communities rather than with them. Top-down deployment. No evidence of engagement with affected populations in design or governance. "Rescue" framing — technology as gift from experts to passive recipients.
- Dossier fields: beneficiary_population, participatory_design, user_engagement, accountability_mechanisms, theory_of_change

### 4. Ethical infrastructure and accountability
- Weight: medium (15 pts)
- Why Asil: MSF ethics committee member — formal institutional ethics governance role. Personal website frames work at "the intersection of data, ethics and impact." She teaches on data ethics and institutional transformation. Her MPhil is in International Development and her BA in Anthropology of Religion — disciplines that foreground ethical frameworks.
- High score: Project has explicit ethical governance — data protection frameworks, consent mechanisms, ethics review processes, transparency about limitations and risks, accountability to affected populations. Formal ethical infrastructure, not just good intentions.
- Low score: Project collects or processes sensitive data without documented ethical review. No consent framework. No transparency about data use or algorithmic decision-making. No accountability mechanisms.
- Dossier fields: data_ethics, accountability_mechanisms, transparency, governance_model, ethical_review

### 5. Conflict/fragile-state applicability
- Weight: medium (10 pts)
- Why Asil: Started in aid sector in Yemen (2009). Co-founded Sana'a Center for Strategic Studies during active conflict. Decade of MSF work across conflict zones (Syria, Yemen, South Africa, Sudan). Advised Sudan's Transitional Government post-revolution. She knows what it takes to build and operate in contested, fragile, or post-conflict environments.
- High score: Project is designed for or has demonstrated deployment in conflict zones, fragile states, or post-crisis contexts. Addresses the specific constraints of these environments: infrastructure limitations, security risks, displaced populations, collapsed institutions. Projects that build institutional capacity in fragile contexts.
- Low score: Project assumes stable infrastructure, functioning institutions, and peaceful contexts. Cannot operate where governance is contested or collapsed.
- Dossier fields: geographic_scope, deployment_context, beneficiary_population, implementation_maturity, conflict_sensitivity

### 6. Movement-building and collective action infrastructure
- Weight: medium (5 pts)
- Why Asil: Diverse Minds podcast: advocacy must be "connected to social movements." She co-founded a think tank (collective knowledge infrastructure) and has coordinated advocacy across 30+ countries (movement-scale coordination). Her personal website lists "BLM UK Sudan" as a project — suggesting engagement with transnational social movements.
- High score: Project enables collective action, coalition-building, or movement coordination. Infrastructure for organisers, campaigners, or civil society networks. Tools that help movements scale or sustain.
- Low score: Project serves individual users without collective dimension. Consumer-focused tools. No movement-building utility.
- Dossier fields: movement_building, collective_action, civic_engagement, coalition_support, theory_of_change

### 7. Epistemic humility and self-criticality
- Weight: low (5 pts)
- Why Asil: Diverse Minds podcast: discussed privilege creating blind spots. Her decolonial framing inherently requires acknowledging the limits of external knowledge. MSF ethics committee work requires systematic engagement with uncertainty and risk. "Promoting humanitarianism as solidarity rather than rescue" implies awareness that the helper's perspective is not automatically correct.
- High score: Project explicitly acknowledges limitations, documents failures, publishes honest assessments of what works and what does not. Evidence of learning from affected communities. Adaptive methodology.
- Low score: Project presents itself as a comprehensive solution without acknowledging constraints. No documentation of limitations or failures. Solutionist framing.
- Dossier fields: epistemic_humility, self_assessment, documentation_of_limitations, learning_mechanisms

---

**Total: 100 pts across 7 criteria.**
**Weight distribution: 3 high (65 pts), 2 medium (25 pts), 1 medium-low (5 pts), 1 low (5 pts).**
