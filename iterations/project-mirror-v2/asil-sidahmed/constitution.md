## Part B: Value Modifiers

### M1. Global South leadership boost (+10-15 pts)
- Direction: boost
- Applies when: Project led by, governed by, or primarily accountable to Global South communities — not merely deployed there.

### M2. Surveillance and extraction penalty (-10-15 pts)
- Direction: reduce
- Applies when: Project collects sensitive data from vulnerable populations without consent frameworks, community data ownership, or accountability. Enables state surveillance without accountability. Data flows from marginalised to powerful without reciprocal benefit.

### M3. Fragile-context resilience boost (+5-10 pts)
- Direction: boost
- Applies when: Project demonstrates ability to operate under conflict-zone constraints — intermittent connectivity, security risks, displaced populations, collapsed institutions.

### M4. Paternalism and rescue-framing penalty (-5-10 pts)
- Direction: reduce
- Applies when: Project frames itself as "helping" or "saving" without evidence of community agency, co-design, or self-determination. Technology-as-gift framing.

### M5. Women and gender-marginalised populations boost (+5-10 pts)
- Direction: boost
- Applies when: Project specifically serves women, girls, or gender-marginalised populations; addresses gendered dimensions of technology; tackles sexual/reproductive health rights.

### M6. Institutional transformation over digitisation boost (+2-5 pts)
- Direction: conditional boost
- Applies when: Project transforms governance or power relations rather than merely digitising existing processes.

**Net modifier cap: +/-20 pts.** No project receives more than +20 or -20 from modifiers alone.

## Part C: Procedural Rules

### Abstention threshold
N/A when dossier lacks purpose + beneficiary population + geographic context (minimum 3 of 7 criteria unevaluable). Thin dossiers with at least purpose + beneficiary + context can be scored with low confidence.

### Prototype handling
Prototypes receive partial protection from maturity penalties, conditional on evidence of community engagement or co-design. Prototypes without beneficiary input are not protected.

### Popularity discount
High visibility is not evidence of quality. Low visibility does not reduce scores. Mainstream media coverage and user numbers are treated as noise, not signal.

### Tie-breaking
Equal scores resolved by: (1) most marginalised population served, (2) most constrained operating context, (3) strongest community governance evidence.

### Uncertainty handling
Thin evidence triggers uncertainty floor (Part D), not score reduction. Thin evidence is not a negative signal — may reflect difficult operating context. Exception: thin evidence with contradictory signals is scored down.

### Novelty vs implementation
Novel, community-grounded projects with no deployment can score up to 60% of maximum on implementation-dependent criteria. Novel projects without community grounding cannot compensate.

### Movement infrastructure vs direct service
Evaluated equally by default. Movement infrastructure serving decolonial, feminist, or conflict-resolution movements receives slight preference (+2-3 pts).

### Scope of concern
Equity lens, not scale lens. 500 served in conflict zone with no alternatives may outscore 5 million in stable OECD context. South-South knowledge transfer valued over Northern model export.

## Scoring Algorithm

```
base_score = sum of criterion scores (0-100)

# Apply modifiers (capped at +/-20)
modifier_total = sum of applicable modifiers
modifier_total = max(-20, min(20, modifier_total))

raw_score = base_score + modifier_total

# Apply underdog protection floor
if dossier_completeness < 0.4:
    final_score = max(30, raw_score)
    flag = "under-researched"
else:
    final_score = raw_score
```

## Synthesis Notes

### Contradictions identified and resolved

**1. Health equity primacy vs. political technology scope**
The highest-weighted criterion (health equity, 25 pts) is domain-specific. Many projects on the 321-longlist have no health dimension. Resolution: projects without health relevance score 0 on this criterion but are not penalised further. The constitution allows a project scoring 0 on health equity but maximising other criteria to reach 75 pts base — still competitive. This is intentional: it reflects Asil's genuine priorities, not a balanced checklist.

**2. Community-centred design vs. thin-dossier evaluation**
Criterion 3 requires participatory design evidence, but many dossiers won't document this. Resolution: for dossiers with completeness >= 0.4, lack of participatory design evidence is a negative signal (scores 0-5). For dossiers below 0.4, the criterion is evaluated based on whatever is available — absence of evidence is not penalised due to underdog protection.

**3. Decolonial governance criterion vs. OECD-context projects**
Criterion 2 (decolonial governance) could structurally disadvantage projects operating in stable OECD contexts. Resolution: the criterion evaluates governance principles (community ownership, power redistribution), not geographic context. An OECD-based project with genuine community governance scores well; a Global South project with extractive governance scores poorly. Geography is handled by modifiers, not criteria.

### Gaps identified

**Technology-specific evaluation weakness:** This constitution evaluates through governance, equity, and ethical lenses. It is weak at evaluating technical quality, scalability, code quality, or technical innovation. This is consistent with Asil's evidence base (no tech-specific evidence found) but means the constitution may undervalue technically excellent projects that lack governance framing. This is a known limitation.

**Open-source position unknown:** No evidence of Asil's views on open-source licensing. The constitution does not include an open-source criterion or modifier. If open-source commitment is important to her (plausible given decolonial values), this constitution under-captures it.

### Operational readiness

All criteria and modifiers can be applied to dossier data. The proposed dossier fields (Part E) would improve scoring precision but are not blocking — the constitution can function with existing fields by inferring from sector, geographic_scope, and beneficiary_population. The scoring algorithm is fully operational.
