## Clean Evidence for Constitution Building

What is actually known at CONFIRMED or PROBABLE level, organised by theme:

### Theme 1: Accountability structures for algorithmic systems
- CONFIRMED (Code Encounters project; "Automation hesitancy"; "Algorithmic tenancies"): Her research repeatedly shows that algorithmic systems in housing operate **without reciprocal accountability mechanisms** — landlords and platforms have power to exclude, but tenants have no redress. The "ordinal tenant" paper documents how complex tenants (young, migrant, adverse-credit) are systematically excluded with no appeal.
- CONFIRMED ("Automation hesitancy"): Human override of algorithmic recommendations ("automation hesitancy") is not straightforwardly positive — it is only valuable when humans are accountable for their overrides. The framing explicitly rejects naive "human in the loop" celebration.
- CONFIRMED (consistent across 4+ papers): Her core political commitment is: **algorithmic systems embedded in public services require accountability structures that currently don't exist**. This is not a general AI-ethics position — it is specific to the structural conditions under which algorithms produce exclusion in housing, credit, and benefit contexts.

### Theme 2: Ethnographic and qualitative methods as accountability tools
- CONFIRMED ("Valuing the manual"; "Liquid homeownership"; "Landlords' and tenants' pathways"): Her methodology is consistently ethnographic and qualitative — in-depth interviews, life-history interviewing, visual elicitation. This is not just a method preference; it is an epistemological commitment: **quantitative or purely technical framings of algorithmic systems cannot capture the lived experience of exclusion**.
- CONFIRMED (EASA 2026 abstract): Ongoing interest in "translation work" and how frontline bureaucrats mediate between state categories and lived realities — algorithms are not neutral operators, they embody categorical choices.
- PROBABLE (career arc from sociology/anthropology): The methodological commitment predates the housing/AI work — it begins with her Bucharest sociology training and the medical sociology phase. This is a durable intellectual identity, not a situational tool choice.

### Theme 3: Communities affected by algorithmic systems must be included in design
- CONFIRMED ("Algorithmic tenancies"): The paper explicitly identifies who is excluded — "complex tenants" (young people, migrants, those with adverse credit) — and argues that digital tenant referencing reproduces and deepens existing disadvantage patterns without accountability for this.
- CONFIRMED (Code Encounters research objectives, Nuffield project page): The project was designed around documenting the experience of people on the receiving end of algorithmic housing decisions, not just the technology itself. Community experience is the unit of analysis.
- PROBABLE (LinkedIn self-description as "social anthropologist interested in data and digital technology"): The anthropological framing centres on communities as the primary analytical lens.

### Theme 4: Human-centred design and co-design orientation

- CONFIRMED (bio provided to Newspeak House): Claims specialisms in "human-centred design," "co-design," and "democratic participation" as current framings. These are self-described, treat as authoritative about self-perception even where unsupported by written work.
- WEAK (no public writing from MoJ period confirms these specific practices at work): The co-design and democratic participation claims in the bio are asserted, not evidenced from the current role.

### Theme 5: Scepticism of empowerment framings that mask surveillance
