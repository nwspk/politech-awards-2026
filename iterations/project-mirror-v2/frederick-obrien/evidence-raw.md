### Evidence summary

**Clearest signal — values and priorities:**

Frederick O'Brien's career is held together by a consistent thread: technology should extend human capability, not replace it, and access to professional-grade tools should not require money or institutional gateways. This manifests in four distinct but related areas.

First, open-access infrastructure for marginalised practitioners. teeline.online gives journalism trainees free, interactive shorthand learning at a time when NCTJ diplomas require it for qualification — he built it from his own revision flashcards, made the source open, and maintains it without charging users. Dummy provides independent and community news outlets with free publishing infrastructure explicitly designed for "shoestring budgets," challenging dependence on corporate platforms. The Gonzo Engineering resources repository is biased explicitly toward free and open-source tools.

Second, equitable economics for creative workers. Soli directly addresses streaming royalty structures that undercompensate musicians — the GitHub description is blunt ("Music streaming that doesn't fuck musicians") and the architecture implements transparent, direct, weekly artist payroll via micropayments. His work at Audioxide includes attention to government-led working groups on streaming royalty inequities. This is not abstract concern: he has built a working alternative.

Third, community journalism as democratic infrastructure. His time at Social Streets CIC (a community interest company using journalism to increase local participation in east London) and at Roman Road LDN doing east London community journalism represents a pre-tech-career grounding in hyperlocal, community-serving journalism. His teaching role at News Associates (NCTJ training) extends this — he is not just a practitioner but someone who invests in training the next cohort of journalists.

Fourth, design ethics and craft. His Smashing Magazine editorial work spans accessibility, the case for an ad-free web, semantic web development, and historical design philosophy (da Vinci, Vitruvius, Saul Bass). These are not just professional interests — they point to someone who thinks carefully about the ethics of how things are made and for whom.

**What is ambiguous:**

The bio's framing ("technology should serve keen minds, not replace them") is explicitly stated as a value but appears more in the character of the projects than in formal published essays. There is limited long-form writing directly addressing AI or platform politics — his views on AI in journalism, for example, are inferred from project ethos rather than directly stated. His Guardian work (interactive journalism, data visualisation) is known institutionally but specific bylines are inaccessible due to Guardian site restrictions. His Smashing Magazine articles are confirmed in form but individual article content could not be fully retrieved.

**What is missing:**

Long-form published writing expressing a worked-out political philosophy of technology is the main gap. The projects express values consistently but he has not (as far as can be found) published a manifesto, longread, or talk that makes the philosophy fully explicit. His east London community journalism archive (Roman Road LDN / Social Streets) is largely inaccessible due to domain migration. His Guardian interactive journalism bylines are inaccessible due to site fetch restrictions.
