# Evaluative Constitution — Part A: Project Criteria
## Evaluator: Frederick O'Brien

---

### Criterion 1: Free and open access to tools — removal of access barriers
- **Weight:** high (max 20 pts)
- **Why Frederick:** The single most consistent value across his entire public record. teeline.online removes the financial barrier to a required professional skill for journalism trainees. Gonzo Engineering's mission statement is explicitly "free, open source tools for the benefit of journalists, musicians, and other members of the freak kingdom." All Gonzo Engineering projects are MIT licensed. The resources repository states an explicit "bias towards free and open source things." This is not an abstract principle — it is embodied in every project he maintains. [gonzo.engineering, teeline.online About, GitHub: resources, GitHub: soli, GitHub: dummy — all CONFIRMED]
- **High score:** Projects with genuine open access (free to use, open-source codebase, no paywalls or premium tiers for core functionality) that serve practitioners or communities who would otherwise be priced out or locked out. Ideally: tools built for people who cannot afford professional alternatives, or that remove an institutional gatekeeping mechanism.
- **Low score:** Projects behind paywalls, subscription models, or premium tiers. Projects that are technically "open" but practically inaccessible (complex deployment, no documentation, prohibitive licensing). Projects that serve organisations with existing resources and alternatives.
- **Dossier fields:** open_source, github_url, licence, access_model, pricing_notes, communities_served, primary_users_or_beneficiaries

---

### Criterion 2: Direct benefit to practitioners and communities — not organisations or platforms
- **Weight:** high (max 20 pts)
- **Why Frederick:** The architecture of his projects consistently routes value to individuals at the end of the chain rather than to intermediary platforms. Soli is explicitly built so that musicians — not labels, not streaming companies — receive fair, direct compensation. Dummy is built so that community journalists — not corporate publishing platforms — control their own distribution. teeline.online is built for journalism trainees, not for NCTJ itself. The Gonzo Engineering mission addresses "journalists, musicians, and other members of the freak kingdom" — practitioners, not institutions. [GitHub: soli README, GitHub: dummy README, teeline.online About — all CONFIRMED]
- **High score:** Projects where the primary economic or practical beneficiary is the individual practitioner, community member, or historically marginalised group — not the platform, institution, or intermediary through which they are served. Especially strong if the project explicitly cuts out an extractive intermediary.
- **Low score:** Projects primarily serving government departments, large organisations, well-resourced NGOs, or institutional actors who are already empowered. Projects where benefits to end-users are secondary to institutional efficiency. Projects that strengthen platforms rather than reducing dependence on them.
- **Dossier fields:** primary_users_or_beneficiaries, communities_served, economic_model, project_type, government_partnerships

---

### Criterion 3: Anti-extraction stance — independence from corporate platforms and surveillance infrastructure
- **Weight:** high (max 20 pts)
- **Why Frederick:** Platform independence is a structural design principle across all his major projects. Dummy explicitly "bypasses tech giants" and "shifts relationships to readers/communities" rather than to platforms. Soli uses IPFS for decentralised file storage, explicitly avoiding centralised corporate streaming infrastructure. The Gonzo Engineering site describes the web as "decadent and depraved," consistent with a critique of extractive platform economics. Smashing Magazine writing lists "towards an ad-free web" as a named topic. [GitHub: dummy README, GitHub: soli README, gonzo.engineering, frederickobrien.com writing — CONFIRMED/PROBABLE]
- **High score:** Projects with open architectures, data portability, and no dependence on corporate APIs or infrastructure. Projects that actively enable users to leave platforms or bypass extractive intermediaries. Projects with explicit commitments against surveillance, data brokering, or advertising-based monetisation.
- **Low score:** Projects built on top of corporate platform APIs (Twitter, Facebook, Google) without offering an exit path. Projects that centralise data unnecessarily. Projects that use surveillance infrastructure, behavioural advertising, or aggregate personal data in ways that benefit the project operator rather than the user. Any project associated with a major tech monopoly.
- **Dossier fields:** open_source, privacy_stance, data_governance_notes, infrastructure_notes, platform_dependencies, advertising_model

### Criterion 4: Community journalism and democratic participation infrastructure
- **Weight:** medium (max 12 pts)
- **Why Frederick:** A year at Social Streets CIC doing community journalism in east London, work at Roman Road LDN, and data journalism teaching at News Associates represent concrete investment in journalism as a vehicle for local democratic participation. His bio explicitly frames community journalism as "the foundation of local democracy." Dummy is directly built to serve grassroots and community-focused news publishers. [socialstreets.co, frederickobrien.com career, GitHub: dummy README — CONFIRMED]
- **High score:** Projects that directly enable community-level democratic participation: hyperlocal journalism infrastructure, community information access tools, civic participation platforms that serve excluded or under-represented communities. Projects that strengthen local democratic capacity rather than national or institutional processes.
- **Low score:** Projects primarily serving professional political actors, national media, or formal government processes. Projects that claim democratic participation benefits but whose primary users are already enfranchised professionals.
- **Dossier fields:** communities_served, issue_area, geography, political_relevance_summary, primary_users_or_beneficiaries

---

### Criterion 5: Technology in service of human skill — tools that augment, not replace
- **Weight:** medium (max 12 pts)
- **Why Frederick:** teeline.online is built to help humans learn a skill — shorthand — not to automate away the need for the skill. The Gonzo Engineering GitHub description states: "technology should support human intelligence rather than replace it." His bio is explicit: "technology should serve keen minds, not replace them." This is the most clearly stated philosophical position in his public record. [GitHub: gonzo-engineering org description, teeline.online, bio — CONFIRMED]
- **High score:** Projects that measurably increase human capacity, literacy, or skill — tools that make their users more capable rather than substituting for their judgment or expertise. Projects in civic literacy, data journalism education, digital skills access, participatory sense-making.
- **Low score:** Projects that automate civic or political processes in ways that remove human judgment or reduce human understanding of decisions. Automated content generation tools. Algorithmic systems that produce decisions without explanation or contestability.
- **Dossier fields:** project_type, scraped_description, use_case_description, tagline, automation_notes

---

### Criterion 6: Design ethics and intentionality — craft as a political choice
- **Weight:** low (max 6 pts)
- **Why Frederick:** His Smashing Magazine editorial work spans accessibility, ad-free web design, semantic web, and design philosophy (da Vinci, Vitruvius, Saul Bass). The Gonzo Engineering tagline ("The Web Is Decadent and Depraved") combines Hunter S. Thompson irreverence with design criticism. Making websites "easier to talk to" (accessibility) is a named topic. This is someone for whom how a tool is made — who it includes, how it speaks, whether it respects the user — is itself a political and ethical choice. [frederickobrien.com writing section, gonzo.engineering — CONFIRMED/PROBABLE]
- **High score:** Projects with demonstrably thoughtful design — explicit accessibility commitments, clear user research, readable by non-experts, without dark patterns or manipulative UX. Projects where design choices clearly reflect awareness of power and inclusion.
- **Low score:** Projects with poor accessibility, no consideration of non-expert users, or design that implicitly excludes non-technical users. Projects that are functional but exclusionary in their UX.
- **Dossier fields:** accessibility_notes, design_notes, target_audience, usability_notes

> Note: Six criteria is appropriate for this evidence base. No criterion for government integration or regulatory clarity is included — the evidence does not support strong weighting here. Frederick's work operates beneath or alongside institutional structures rather than trying to reshape them through policy. His Guardian role (software engineer at editorial/tech overlap) does not yield direct evidence of policy engagement. No criterion on global/international reach — his work is deeply UK and practitioner-specific; cross-jurisdictional scale is not evidenced as a priority.
