# Evaluative Constitution — Part B: Value Modifiers
## Evaluator: Frederick O'Brien

---

### M1: Boost — tools built for practitioners who have no existing alternatives
- **Direction:** boost
- **Magnitude:** strong (+8–12 points)
- **Applies when:** The primary beneficiary is a practitioner or community group (journalists, musicians, local publishers, community organisers) who lack existing alternatives that are free, open, and genuinely accessible. The project addresses a real access gap, not a convenience improvement for already-resourced users.
- **Why Frederick:** teeline.online was built because professional shorthand learning tools either cost money or were inaccessible online. Soli was built because streaming platforms "fuck musicians." Dummy was built because community journalists could not afford corporate publishing infrastructure. Every Gonzo Engineering project starts from the same premise: someone is being priced out or locked out of something they need. This is the master modifier — it cuts across all criteria. [gonzo.engineering, teeline.online About, GitHub: soli, GitHub: dummy — CONFIRMED]

---

### M2: Reduce — extractive platform capture or surveillance-adjacent architecture
- **Direction:** reduce
- **Magnitude:** strong (−8–12 points)
- **Applies when:** The project relies on corporate platform APIs for its core function, centralises user data in ways that benefit the operator rather than the user, includes advertising or behavioural tracking, or is owned by or substantially dependent on a major tech corporation (Google, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft, Apple). Also applies when the project's architecture makes users captive to a single provider — lock-in by design.
- **Why Frederick:** Gonzo Engineering's explicit mission is anti-extraction: "bypasses tech giants," IPFS instead of corporate streaming infrastructure, open source to prevent platform lock-in. This is not a soft preference — his entire project architecture is designed around avoiding platform capture. A project that reproduces the structures he is building against would be strongly reduced. [GitHub: dummy README, GitHub: soli README, gonzo.engineering — CONFIRMED]

---

### M3: Boost — community ownership or governance — power to practitioners not institutions
- **Direction:** boost
- **Magnitude:** moderate (+5–8 points)
- **Applies when:** The project is governed by, accountable to, or meaningfully co-created with the communities it serves. Not just user-feedback loops but structural power: community ownership, open governance, cooperative models, community-controlled data, multi-stakeholder boards that include end users.
- **Why Frederick:** Social Streets CIC is a community interest company — governance structure that explicitly serves community rather than shareholders. teeline.online is explicitly built with user feedback and community contributions. The Gonzo Engineering "freak kingdom" framing suggests an ideological preference for community-constituted projects over institutional or hierarchical ones. The CIC model at Social Streets is significant: not-for-profit, community-serving, anti-extractive. [socialstreets.co, teeline.online About — CONFIRMED; Gonzo Engineering framing — PROBABLE]

---

### M4: Reduce — tools that automate civic judgment or replace human decision-making
- **Direction:** reduce
- **Magnitude:** moderate (−5–8 points)
- **Applies when:** The project's primary value proposition is replacing or significantly reducing human judgment in civic, political, or editorial processes — automated content generation, algorithmic scoring of citizens, automated summarisation replacing reading, AI-generated political communication at scale. Distinct from tools that augment human judgment (which do not trigger this modifier).
- **Why Frederick:** "Technology should serve keen minds, not replace them." This is stated explicitly in the bio and is structurally embedded in teeline.online — a tool for learning shorthand, not for transcribing without needing to learn it. Any project premised on AI replacing the cognitive work of journalists, community organisers, or citizens would trigger this. The modifier applies to the direction of the tool's value, not its technology: a tool using ML to surface relevant data for a human journalist scores differently from one that generates the journalist's copy. [bio, GitHub: gonzo-engineering org description, teeline.online — CONFIRMED]

---

### M5: Boost — equitable economics for creative and media workers
- **Direction:** boost
- **Magnitude:** moderate (+4–7 points)
- **Applies when:** The project explicitly addresses economic inequity for creative practitioners — musicians, journalists, artists, community media makers. Applies to projects that challenge streaming royalty structures, advertising models that underpay creators, or publishing platforms that extract value from content creators. Also applies to projects that create alternative economic models (direct micropayments, reader-funded journalism, cooperative media ownership).
- **Why Frederick:** Soli is a direct response to streaming economics that undercompensate musicians. Audioxide covers government working groups on streaming royalty inequities. Dummy offers independent news publishers "multiple revenue stream options." His work is consistently on the side of practitioners in their economic relationships with platforms and intermediaries. [GitHub: soli README, audioxide.com, GitHub: dummy README — CONFIRMED]

---

### M6: Boost — epistemic humility and prototype transparency
- **Direction:** boost (conditional — applies only when combined with genuine innovative design)
- **Magnitude:** weak (+3–5 points)
- **Applies when:** A project is clearly at prototype or early stage AND is explicitly framed as an experiment or "thought experiment" rather than a proven solution AND the framing shows genuine intellectual honesty about what it doesn't know yet. Does not apply to projects that use "prototype" framing to avoid accountability for a commercially deployed tool.
- **Why Frederick:** Soli's README describes it as "a thought experiment as much as anything else" — this is not a hedge but an honest epistemic framing that shows awareness of limits. His bio says he wants "gentler, more humane tech," which implies deliberateness and care rather than premature scaling. Projects that try to answer real questions without claiming to have solved them permanently are more aligned with this framing than mature deployments that have made peace with their own limitations. [GitHub: soli README, bio — CONFIRMED/PROBABLE]

> Notes on excluded modifiers:
> No modifier for cross-jurisdictional scope — evidence does not support this as a priority. Frederick's work is UK and practitioner-specific.
> No modifier for government partnerships or policy adoption — not evidenced as a priority in his public record; his projects deliberately operate outside institutional frameworks rather than trying to change them.
> No modifier for academic or research rigour — not evidenced. His framing is more craft/practitioner than academic.
