## Clean Evidence for Constitution Building

What is actually known at CONFIRMED or PROBABLE level, organised by theme:

### Theme 1: Empowerment of excluded and marginalised communities
- CONFIRMED (Xinhua 2018): Co-founded Social Hackers Academy in Athens teaching refugees from Syria, Afghanistan and other conflict zones to code. Direct quote: "The most difficult part is reassuring people that they can do it...And the most rewarding is being there at the moment when they realize they can do it."
- PROBABLE (bio, CYF): Continued refugee coding education at CodeYourFuture in UK (2019-2021), which teaches full-stack web development to refugees and people from disadvantaged backgrounds.
- CONFIRMED (Sigma Labs): Current role at social mobility B-Corp focused on technical education — specifically serving populations excluded from tech careers by socioeconomic background.
- INFERENCE: This is the dominant durable value in Owen's career. Every career move from The Guardian onwards has been toward teaching excluded populations to code. The consistency is strong and spans 8+ years.

### Theme 2: Education and capability-building as core mechanism
- CONFIRMED (Xinhua 2018): The quote frames the reward as the moment learners "realize they can do it" — this is an empowerment-through-capability frame, not a service-delivery frame. The value is in the transformation of the person, not the output.
- CONFIRMED (career arc): SHA → CYF → Sigma Labs represents a sustained focus on education as the primary mechanism for social change. Not advocacy, not infrastructure, not policy — teaching people to code.
- INFERENCE: Owen's theory of change is: give excluded people technical skills → they gain agency and economic opportunity. This is education-first, not policy-first or infrastructure-first.

### Theme 3: Volunteer-driven, low-cost, resource-efficient models
- CONFIRMED (Xinhua 2018): SHA operated at ~$5,000 per graduate with donated office space and volunteer teachers. This is dramatically lower than formal education costs.
- PROBABLE (CYF): CodeYourFuture is volunteer-run, teaching full-stack development with volunteer mentors.
- CONFIRMED (career trajectory): Owen left well-paid commercial engineering (The Guardian) for volunteer-driven education work. The personal sacrifice validates that low-cost, volunteer models are not just pragmatic but valued.
- INFERENCE: Resource efficiency and volunteer-driven delivery are valued, not just tolerated. Projects that achieve impact with minimal institutional overhead would score well.

### Theme 4: Open-source code and replicable educational materials
- CONFIRMED (GitHub): Active open-source contributor. Project-Polymorph is on GitHub. Multiple public repositories.
- PROBABLE (SHA/CYF model): Both organisations teach web development using open-source tools and frameworks. The educational model itself is designed to be replicable — CYF has chapters in multiple UK cities.
- INFERENCE: Open-source is valued both as a tool and as a model. Projects that open-source their educational materials or enable others to replicate would align.

### Theme 5: Practical deployment with real users
- CONFIRMED (SHA): Real students, real graduates, documented cost per outcome. Not a concept — a functioning programme.
- CONFIRMED (career arc): Owen's career is entirely hands-on — building software, then building educational programmes. No academic or think-tank periods.
- INFERENCE: Implementation and real-world use matter more than theoretical design. Projects with real users would score higher than concept papers.

### Theme 6: Political technology connecting to civic participation
- CONFIRMED (GitHub): Project-Polymorph described as "political technology" — a Matrix client with civic framing.
- CONFIRMED (Newspeak House): Fellowship at a political technology institution.
- INFERENCE ONLY: The political technology interest is real but thin in evidence. The GitHub project and fellowship are the only signals. No writing or talks on political technology exist. This theme is weighted LOW because the evidence base is shallow compared to the education themes.

### Theme 7: Scalability and cross-context replicability
- PROBABLE (SHA → CYF transition): The SHA model in Athens was replicated/adapted as CYF in the UK, suggesting Owen values models that can be transported across contexts.
- CONFIRMED (CYF): Multi-city chapters demonstrate scaling through replication rather than centralised growth.
- INFERENCE: Models that scale through trained people (teachers, facilitators, volunteers) rather than through technology alone would align with Owen's approach.

## Constitution Building Guidance

**What the constitution CAN confidently capture:**
- Strong orientation toward empowerment of excluded/marginalised communities through education
- Preference for volunteer-driven, low-cost, resource-efficient models
- Preference for open-source and replicable approaches
- Value placed on practical deployment over theoretical design
- General orientation toward coding education as a mechanism for social change

**What the constitution CANNOT confidently capture:**
- How Owen evaluates non-education approaches to inclusion (e.g., participatory democracy platforms)
- His specific views on political technology projects
- Whether he values advocacy, policy, or infrastructure approaches alongside education
- How his views have evolved during the Newspeak House fellowship
- Any nuanced distinction between different types of civic tech

**Critical gap:** The constitution will over-index on education because that is where the evidence is thickest. This is an honest reflection of the evidence but may not accurately represent Owen's full evaluative lens, especially given his current engagement with political technology at Newspeak House.
