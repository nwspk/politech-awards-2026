## Evidence Summary

**Paragraph 1 — Career arc and core identity**
Chris Owen's public record documents a career arc from computer science graduate through commercial software engineering to technical education focused on excluded populations. He studied Computer Science at Lancaster University, then worked as a software engineer at agencies (Demopad/Nimbletank) where he did client work for brands including Sky Sports and Nando's — these were agency clients, not direct employment. He then moved to The Guardian as a software engineer. The pivotal career shift came in 2017 when he co-founded Social Hackers Academy in Athens, Greece, teaching refugees to code at approximately $5,000 per graduate. He subsequently joined CodeYourFuture in the UK (2019-2021), another coding school for refugees and disadvantaged groups, before becoming Partner: Technical Performance at Sigma Labs, a social mobility B-Corp focused on technical education and assessment.

**Paragraph 2 — Values signal from the record**
The clearest signal in Owen's career is a sustained commitment to teaching coding to people excluded from the technology sector — specifically refugees and economically disadvantaged populations. His only confirmed first-person quote (Xinhua 2018) articulates this precisely: "The most difficult part is reassuring people that they can do it...And the most rewarding is being there at the moment when they realize they can do it." This is an empowerment-through-education frame: the value is not in the code produced but in the transformation of the learner's self-belief and capability. The career move from well-paid commercial engineering (The Guardian) to volunteer-driven refugee education (SHA) and then to a social mobility B-Corp (Sigma Labs) is a coherent values trajectory.

**Paragraph 3 — Political technology interest**
Owen's GitHub contains Project-Polymorph, described as "A glossy Matrix collaboration client for the web -- but it's political technology." This is the only direct evidence of engagement with political technology as a concept. His Newspeak House fellowship places him in a political technology community. However, there is no public writing, talks, podcasts, or social media commentary on political technology. The political technology dimension of his identity is recent and thin compared to the education dimension.

**Paragraph 4 — What is not known**
There is a near-total absence of public voice. No blog posts. No conference talks. No podcast appearances. No published writing. No accessible social media. The entire constitution must be built from career pattern inference plus one 2018 quote. This is the most significant limitation of this evidence base. Owen appears to be someone who does rather than writes about doing — which is consistent with the hands-on education work but means the constitution has no direct evidence of how he evaluates others' work, what he thinks about specific political technology projects, or how his views have evolved during his Newspeak House fellowship.

**Paragraph 5 — Bio corrections**
Two factual clarifications from the evidence: (1) "Sky Sports, Nando's" in the bio are agency clients from his Demopad/Nimbletank period, not direct employers; (2) "Head of Technical Education at startup" maps to "Partner: Technical Performance" at Sigma Labs — a slightly different title than described.
