## Synthesis Notes

### Contradictions found and resolved

**Contradiction 1: Prototype protection vs Self-Hosting scoring**
Criterion 2 rewards projects that are *actually* self-hostable with documented deployment. Prototype protection (Part C) suspends deployment evidence when completeness is low. These pull against each other for early-stage, open-source-but-not-yet-deployed tools.

*Resolution:* Criterion 2 distinguishes between two things — architectural design (can this be self-hosted?) and deployment evidence (is it being self-hosted?). Under prototype protection, only the deployment evidence half is suspended. A project with a clearly self-hostable, open-source architecture scores up to 15/20 on Criterion 2 even without deployment evidence; the remaining 5 points require some deployment evidence. This is a meaningful distinction that prevents the criterion from collapsing entirely for early-stage projects while preserving the deployment signal for mature ones.

**Contradiction 2: Modifier 6 (under-resourced boost) vs Modifier 3 (extraction penalty)**
A VC-backed project that also happens to be early-stage could theoretically trigger both M6 (boost) and M3 (penalty). These cancel in the worst case, but the intent is clearly that M3 overrides M6.

*Resolution:* Modifier 3 takes precedence when it fires. If a project is VC-backed with investor exit incentives, it does not receive M6 even if it is also early-stage. The framing is: under-resourced work deserves protection *because* it has not chosen the extraction path, not as a general size discount that applies regardless of funding model.

**Contradiction 3: Underdog floor vs Modifier 3 penalty**
The underdog floor sets a minimum of 28 points. Modifier 3 can push scores down by 10–15 points. If a VC-backed project has dossier_completeness < 0.4, does the floor apply?

*Resolution:* The floor does not apply when Modifier 3 fires at maximum magnitude. A VC-backed, extractive project with thin dossier is not penalised for being obscure — it is penalised for being structurally incompatible with the constitution. These are different failure modes. The floor is for projects whose constitutional fit is genuinely unknown; it is not a minimum score guarantee for projects whose fit is known to be poor.

### Gaps identified

**Gap 1: Anti-metric innovation (from Criterion 3)**
The constitution has a strong anti-metric critique (from "Spreadsheets, Souls, and Fragile Futures") but the dossier fields used to operationalise Criterion 3 are all qualitative text fields. The anti-metric stance is hard to score from keyword extraction alone — it requires reading the project's theory of change carefully. This reduces scoring consistency across 321 projects.

**Gap 2: Ecstatic / cultural projects**
Modifier 1 (Ecstatic Technology Boost) is the most distinctive element of this constitution, but it is also the hardest to apply from structured dossier data. "Joyful," "embodied," and "celebrates collective experience" are qualitative judgements. The ranking agent will need to apply Modifier 1 conservatively, reserving it for projects where the dossier explicitly describes cultural, artistic, or embodied dimensions.

**Gap 3: No direct climate / ecological criterion signals in dossier**
Criterion 6 (Environmental Responsibility) requires data that most dossiers do not contain (energy use, lifecycle design). In practice, Criterion 6 will fire primarily for projects that explicitly address climate or ecology as their issue area, and will score near-zero for most projects. This is an acceptable gap — Gamithra would likely endorse the principle that absence of ecological consideration is itself a signal.

### Operational readiness

The constitution is operationally ready for the ranking agent with the following caveats:
- Modifier 1 (Ecstatic boost) requires human-like reading of project character, not just keyword matching. Apply conservatively.
- Criterion 3 (Anti-Extraction) requires judgement about theory of change, not just funding model. Read the `political_relevance_summary` field carefully.
- The extended underdog floor for Global South projects requires geographic identification from the dossier's `geography` or `communities_served` field.
- When Modifier 3 fires AND Modifier 6 would also fire: Modifier 3 takes precedence (see contradiction resolution above).

### Coherence assessment

The constitution reads as a coherent value system from one person. The through-line is consistent: community ownership over corporate control, structural change over service delivery, open protocols over proprietary capture, joyful and embodied over extractive and metric-driven, protection of the under-resourced over reward of the well-documented. A near-perfect project under this constitution would be an open-protocol, community-governed, self-hostable civic infrastructure tool with a cooperative ownership model and some element of cultural joy in its design — and a thin dossier because it's building something new rather than marketing something established. The clearest losers are VC-backed, SaaS, surveillance-adjacent, or engagement-maximising tools regardless of their stated civic purpose.
