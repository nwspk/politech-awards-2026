## What Gamithra Would Champion

- **Federated, self-hostable civic infrastructure** — Tools like Bonfire (#1), Citizen OS (#7), and CommunityRule (#15) that embody the self-hosting and open protocol values at the heart of her constitution. These projects let communities own their digital infrastructure rather than rent it from a platform. Bonfire's federated social networking on ActivityPub is almost a direct instantiation of the "world that self-hosts" pillar.

- **Participatory democracy platforms with real community governance** — adhocracy+ (#5), CONSUL (#6), Decidim (#11), and All Our Ideas (#17) score well because they combine open-source design with genuine participatory governance structures. These are tools for collective decision-making, not just tools *about* it. Her wasa2il fork and Matrix pairwise ranking bot show she builds exactly this kind of tool herself.

- **Anti-extractive, structurally ambitious projects** — Agencies for Good (#3), Cobudget (#8), and Open Council Network (#9) hit the anti-extraction criterion hard. They aim to redistribute decision-making power or financial resources rather than optimising within existing structures. The constitution's highest-weighted criteria (community ownership, technological sovereignty, anti-extraction) all fire here.

- **Ecstatic, embodied, and culturally joyful technology** — Open Heart Mind (#4), Spacetube (#16), and projects with explicit cultural or artistic dimensions benefit from Modifier 1. This is the most distinctive element of her constitution — the "world that raves" pillar that treats dancing, art, and collective joy as political technology. Few other evaluators in the cohort would have this modifier at all.

- **Small-scale, under-resourced, community-built projects** — Modifier 6 (small-scale boost) combined with the underdog protection floor creates a safety net for emerging work. Projects that are early-stage, volunteer-run, or working outside the dominant civic tech ecosystem get structural protection rather than penalisation for being less well-documented.

## What Gamithra Would Discount

- **VC-backed tools regardless of stated civic purpose** — Bluesky (#315), DoNotPay (#318), Yoti (#321), and Collab.Land (#316) all receive severe M3 penalties. The constitution treats venture capital funding as structurally incompatible with community sovereignty — "innovation that concentrates wealth is anti-political by design." Even projects with genuine civic features get hammered if their funding model implies investor exit incentives.

- **Surveillance-adjacent or attention-extractive platforms** — Modifier 5 (addictive design penalty) and Modifier 3 (extraction penalty) stack against tools that use engagement metrics, algorithmic feeds, or behavioural profiling. The Humane Tech Association's anti-addictive-design principle is enacted as a scoring floor.

- **Efficiency optimisation without structural critique** — Projects that make existing systems "work better" without questioning their premises score poorly on Criterion 3. The constitution explicitly distinguishes between "building alternatives" and "managing the brokenness." Government contract management tools, process optimisers, and compliance platforms that serve institutional efficiency land in the lower half.

- **Proprietary SaaS with no self-hosting path** — Criterion 2 (technological sovereignty) actively penalises tools that lock communities into vendor dependency. Cloud-only products with no open-source alternative, no data export, and no self-hosting documentation face a structural ceiling regardless of their civic purpose.

## Constitutional Failure Mode

The most significant failure mode is the **anti-extraction over-correction**. Gamithra's constitution is built around a binary: community-owned/open/sovereign vs. extractive/corporate/proprietary. This binary is principled and well-evidenced from her public record — but it systematically undervalues projects that operate in hybrid spaces. A VC-backed tool that has genuinely shifted power dynamics (e.g., a well-funded open-source platform that achieved government adoption precisely because it had professional support) gets the same M3 penalty as a surveillance platform. The constitution cannot distinguish between "took VC money and is building genuine public infrastructure" and "took VC money and is extracting value."

A second, subtler failure mode is the **ecstatic technology blind spot in reverse**. Modifier 1 (the joyful/embodied boost) is the most distinctive and politically interesting element of this constitution — but it's also the hardest to apply from structured dossier data. Projects that *are* joyful and embodied but don't describe themselves that way in their dossier miss the boost entirely. Meanwhile, projects that use the right aesthetic vocabulary ("creative," "cultural," "festival") may trigger it even when the cultural element is incidental rather than constitutive. The modifier's distinctive power is also its scoring fragility.

Finally, the constitution's strong anti-metric stance (from "Spreadsheets, Souls, and Fragile Futures") creates an ironic tension: the ranking *is* a metric. Every project receives a number. The constitution critiques "measuring what doesn't need improvement" but then measures 321 projects on a 0-100 scale. This is not a bug that can be fixed — it's a structural feature of any quantified evaluation system, and one that Gamithra would likely identify immediately.
