### Criterion 1: Community Ownership and Governance Model
- **Weight:** HIGH (20 points)
- **Why Gamithra:** The clearest through-line in her entire public record is ownership and governance. She explicitly names platform cooperatives, worker cooperatives, and community land trusts as preferred models [mission.gamithra.com, 2025]. TVÍK's own structure (anti-VC, grant-funded, collectivisation aspiration) enacts this value. The Regovern.Land event (2024) placed her on a panel specifically about alternative governance and ownership structures.
- **High score (80–100):** Project is governed by its users or community (platform cooperative, worker-owned, DAO, community land trust model, municipal ownership); governance documentation is transparent and participatory; users have meaningful decision-making power, not just advisory roles.
- **Low score (0–30):** Project is VC-backed with standard investor governance; community has no ownership stake; decisions rest entirely with a single founder or corporate board; monetisation model treats users as product.
- **Dossier fields used:** `primary_users_or_beneficiaries`, `communities_served`, `political_relevance_summary`, `tagline`, `scraped_description`

### Criterion 2: Technological Sovereignty / Self-Hosting Capability
- **Weight:** HIGH (20 points)
- **Why Gamithra:** "Technological sovereignty, where communities control their essential infrastructure" is stated as a core goal [mission.gamithra.com, 2025]. This includes community-owned internet, energy, and tools with open protocols. Her cybersecurity background (Syndis, NCC Group) and the self-hosting pillar make this not just rhetorical — she understands the technical prerequisites for genuine autonomy. The althingy project (Icelandic parliament data parser) reflects the civic open-data side of this.
- **High score (80–100):** Project is self-hostable (open source, documented deployment, no mandatory cloud dependency); uses open protocols; enables communities to run their own instances without vendor lock-in; designed for infrastructure sovereignty not just convenience use.
- **Low score (0–30):** Proprietary SaaS with no self-hosted option; requires ongoing corporate API access; data stays with vendor; community has no path to independent operation.
- **Dossier fields used:** `scraped_description`, `political_relevance_summary`, `issue_area`, `tagline`

### Criterion 3: Anti-Extraction Innovation Design
- **Weight:** HIGH (20 points)
- **Why Gamithra:** "Innovation that concentrates wealth is anti-political by design" is her explicit framing. The mission statement directly criticises institutional work that "optimises within broken systems rather than building alternatives." The "Spreadsheets, Souls, and Fragile Futures" talk challenges metric-driven innovation. She distinguishes between projects that address systemic causes and those that provide "better ways to manage the brokenness" [mission.gamithra.com, 2025].
- **High score (80–100):** Project explicitly addresses structural causes of political exclusion or wealth concentration; theory of change goes beyond service delivery to system change; does not primarily serve to make existing power structures more efficient; process is participatory and shapes the product.
- **Low score (0–30):** Project optimises existing systems without questioning their premises (e.g., better surveillance management, more efficient means-testing); concentrates data/power/revenue in a single actor; justifies impact through efficiency metrics alone.
- **Dossier fields used:** `political_relevance_summary`, `scraped_description`, `issue_area`, `tagline`

### Criterion 4: Privacy by Design and Data Ethics
- **Weight:** MEDIUM (12 points)
- **Why Gamithra:** Co-founded Icelandic Association for Humane Technology, whose principles explicitly include "privacy by design" and strict data protection. The East of Moon podcast frames AI risk as primarily about human political choices, not autonomous systems — which puts data governance squarely in the political domain. This is a value she has institutionalised, not merely stated.
- **High score (80–100):** Privacy by design (data minimisation, no unnecessary collection, user data ownership); no surveillance-adjacent design patterns; transparent data governance; explicit anti-tracking architecture; GDPR-aligned or more protective.
- **Low score (0–30):** Business model depends on data collection or behavioural profiling; no documented data governance; surveillance-enabling design (even if for ostensibly good purposes); opaque algorithmic decision-making with no user recourse.
- **Dossier fields used:** `scraped_description`, `political_relevance_summary`, `issue_area`

### Criterion 5: Accessibility and Inclusive Participation
- **Weight:** MEDIUM (12 points)
- **Why Gamithra:** TVÍK's Equal Opportunities Fund ("we do not want your financial situation to be a barrier") enacts this value concretely. The Humane Tech Association principle of "inclusive participation" addresses digital divides and accessible design. The emotional/human-centred approach to learning (Grapevine interview) reflects a sustained attention to who is excluded from existing systems.
- **High score (80–100):** Actively designed to include populations excluded from mainstream civic tech (low income, non-English speakers, disabled users, low-digital-literacy populations); fee structures or accessibility features actively remove barriers; multilingual or locally adapted.
- **Low score (0–30):** Implicitly designed for already-enfranchised users (tech-literate, affluent, English-speaking); no accessibility features; no evidence of equitable access model; assumes high baseline civic capacity.
- **Dossier fields used:** `communities_served`, `primary_users_or_beneficiaries`, `scraped_description`

### Criterion 6: Environmental Responsibility and Long-Term Thinking
- **Weight:** MEDIUM (12 points)
- **Why Gamithra:** Humane Tech Association Principle 5 explicitly covers ecological impact, renewable resources, sustainable design, and supporting climate solutions. The solarpunk and regenerative futures framing throughout the mission statement treats ecological sustainability as inseparable from political technology. "Protecting future generations" is a named organisational principle [mannvaen.gamithra.com].
- **High score (80–100):** Project explicitly addresses climate, ecological sustainability, or regenerative systems; or is designed with minimal environmental footprint and lifecycle sustainability; addresses long-term consequences not just immediate gains.
- **Low score (0–30):** Significant ecological footprint with no mitigation; driven by short-term efficiency without long-term consideration; or explicitly serves fossil-fuel-adjacent interests.
- **Dossier fields used:** `issue_area`, `scraped_description`, `political_relevance_summary`

### Criterion 7: Open Government and Democratic Transparency
- **Weight:** LOW (6 points)
- **Why Gamithra:** Humane Tech Association Principle 7 covers "open government data, civic engagement tools, and technological solutions enhancing transparency." The althingy project (Icelandic parliament speech parser) directly instantiates this interest. Pirate Party politics also has a strong open-government dimension. This is a genuine value but ranked below the structural ownership/sovereignty/anti-extraction criteria because those are more central to her framing than procedural transparency alone.
- **High score (80–100):** Project directly enhances government transparency, open data publication, or civic accountability mechanisms; open government APIs; parliamentary or administrative data accessible to citizens.
- **Low score (0–30):** Operates without transparency; no public accountability mechanism; or is only marginally related to governance transparency.
- **Dossier fields used:** `political_relevance_summary`, `issue_area`, `scraped_description`
